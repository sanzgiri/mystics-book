## Life

Vinayak Bhave was born on 11 September 1895 in Gagode, in the Kolaba district of Maharashtra, into a Brahmin family. His mother, Rukmini Devi, was devout and influenced him strongly. Wikipedia lists him as the eldest of five children. In 1916, on the way to examinations in Bombay, he is said to have burned his school certificates. He went to Kashi, and on 7 June 1916 he met Gandhi at Kochrab Ashram. Vinoba later said, "Providence took me to Gandhi." He joined the ashram soon after.

Vinoba spent years in Gandhi's ashrams, in study, spinning and silence. He served several prison terms, including six months at Dhulia in 1932. There he lectured on the Gita to fellow prisoners, and the talks were later printed. He also read proofs of *Gitai*, his Marathi verse rendering, there. He wrote *Ishavasyavritti* and *Sthitaprajna Darshan* in jail. Gandhi chose him in 1940 as the first individual satyagrahi of the campaign against Indian participation in the war. In 1938, after illness, he moved to Paramdham Ashram at Paunar near Wardha, which remained his base.

After Gandhi's death in 1948 he became the leading figure of the Sarvodaya movement, the "welfare of all." The Sarvodaya idea took shape at a meeting of Gandhi's followers at Sevagram in March 1948. On 18 April 1951, in Pochampally in Telangana, villagers said they needed eighty acres of land. A landlord, Ram Chandra Reddy, offered a hundred, and Vinoba began to walk from village to village asking for land for the landless. In May 1952 he received a whole village, Mangrath in Uttar Pradesh, as a gift, the first *gramdan*. He later received more than a thousand villages. He died at Paunar on 15 November 1982, after refusing food and medicine for a few days. He received the first Magsaysay Award in 1958 for community leadership and the Bharat Ratna after his death, in 1983.

## The teaching

Vinoba's religious writing centres on the *Bhagavad Gita*. He called his Marathi Gita *Gitai*, "Mother Gita," and by Wikipedia's report he said the Gita is his life's breath. He read it as a teaching for people who must act. He took Arjuna's despair on the battlefield as the human situation in general, and the book's answer as the discipline of doing one's work without clinging to its results. The ideal figure in his reading is the *sthitaprajna*, the person of steady wisdom whom the second chapter describes, and he wrote a book on that figure.

In the 1950s he applied the same reading to land. Asking for land, and receiving it, was for him a practice in giving up possession. Followers describe the donor as giving up a claim and the walker as giving up security. His supporters called this the Gita's selfless action carried into village life. The village gift, or *gramdan*, extended the idea from an acre to a community.

## Critiques and questions

**The Emergency.** Vinoba supported the Emergency of 1975 to 1977, calling it *anushasana parva*, a time for discipline, and opponents coined the phrase *sarkari sant*, "government saint" (Wikipedia). 

**Results.** How much of the land pledged in Bhoodan reached the landless is disputed, and the figures vary by source.
