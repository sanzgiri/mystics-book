## Life

Vittal Rao was born on 10 April 1884 in Hosdurg, on the coast of what is now northern Kerala. His parents, Balakrishna Rao and Lalita Bai, were Saraswat Brahmins. He did badly at school, failing the matriculation exam more than once by one account, and trained in textile engineering in Bombay. He worked as a spinning master in a cotton mill at Gulbarga and moved between mills in southern India. He married Umabai, who took the name Rukmabai at marriage as Saraswat custom required, in 1908 or 1909 (sources differ on the year), and a daughter, Ramabai, was born around 1913.

His own business went badly. In 1919 he opened a dyeing and printing works in Mangalore, and by about 1920 it was failing under debt. He had read Vivekananda, Ramakrishna and Rama Tirtha, and he now turned to the mantra his father had been given by a sannyasi and had passed on to him: *Sri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram*. Vittal Rao added the syllable Om and began to repeat it all day.

On the night of 27 December 1922 he left home, after writing a farewell letter to his wife. He took two lengths of cloth and a few rupees, took the ochre robe of a renunciate and the name Ramdas, "servant of Ram," at Srirangam, and set out as a wandering mendicant. He visited Tiruvannamalai and Ramana Maharshi. By his own account in *In Quest of God*, Ramana blessed him in silence. He then spent three weeks to a month (accounts differ) in a cave on Arunachala, where he reports the first full vision of Ram in everything around him. He travelled to the Himalayas and back. In 1923 his family traced him to Hubli, where he was visiting the teacher Siddharudha Swami, and on Siddharudha's advice he went back with his wife and daughter to Mangalore, though he lived apart from them.

He wrote *In Quest of God* in a cave on the Kadri hills near Mangalore, and it was published in 1925. It made him known across southern India. In 1928 he opened a small ashram at Kasaragod, with a young widow, Krishnabai, among those who came to him. Public criticism followed the presence of a young woman in a renunciant's ashram, by Krishnabai's later recollection, and a night disturbance made him leave. The Kasaragod ashram had also been called Anandashram. Devotees opened a new Anandashram at Kanhangad on 15 May 1931, and he lived there for the rest of his life.

From August 1954 to January 1955 he toured Europe, North America, Japan and Southeast Asia with Krishnabai and a young monk, Swami Satchidananda. He met D. T. Suzuki in Japan and recorded the journey in *World Is God* (1955). He died at Anandashram in July 1963.

## The teaching

Ramdas taught that Ram is the only reality and that the way to Ram is the Name. To repeat it without pause was the whole practice. The mind that repeated it would be purified, and in time the repeater would see Ram in all beings. Ramdas wrote of seeing Ram in people, animals and objects after his time on Arunachala.

He used the word Ram for the impersonal absolute and for the son of Dasaratha at once, and cited Kabir for the idea. This puts him between devotion and Advaita. He asked for surrender, which meant treating everything that happens as the will of Ram, good fortune and bad. The ashram's site gives a line from him on practice: "Spirituality that cannot be applied in actual life is no spirituality."

Service followed. He taught that love shows itself as help to others, and he held that all religions lead to the same goal. Anandashram opened its kitchen to visitors and added a school, a vocational workshop and a hospital, each later handed to other bodies according to the ashram's timeline. Chanting still runs through the day there. Wikipedia's article on the ashram puts the written repetitions of the mantra it has collected, a practice called *likhit japa*, above 75 crore (750 million), without saying when that count was reached.

<aside class="note"><strong>Papa.</strong> Devotees called him Papa, and Krishnabai Mother. The names stayed on the ashram and its publications.</aside>

## Lineage

Ramdas sits in the Ram bhakti current and does not belong to a formal lineage. His father gave him the mantra, and the tree shows no initiation by a guru. He met Ramana Maharshi briefly, an encounter he describes in his own book, and Siddharudha Swami of Hubli gave him advice. Mataji Krishnabai (1903–1989) led Anandashram after his death, followed by Swami Satchidananda (to 2008).
