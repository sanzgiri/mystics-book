## Life

Swami Sarvapriyananda was born Biswarup Mitra in Kolkata in 1971, according to Wikipedia and *The Global Indian*, and grew up in a Bengali family in Bhubaneswar, Odisha. He told the *Harvard Gazette* in 2019 that as a boy he had two ambitions, to become a pilot and to find God, and that the second gradually displaced the first. He took an MBA at the Xavier Institute of Management in Bhubaneswar and in 1994, at twenty-three, joined the Ramakrishna Math and Mission, the monastic order founded by Vivekananda in Ramakrishna's name. He took the final vows of sannyasa in 2004 at Belur Math, the order's headquarters near Kolkata.

His work in India was mostly as a teacher. He was an acharya, or instructor, at the training centre for young monks at Belur Math, vice principal of the order's school at Deoghar, principal of its teacher-training college at Belur, and, according to *The Global Indian*, the first registrar of the Ramakrishna Mission's university at Belur. In December 2015 he was sent to Los Angeles as assistant minister of the Vedanta Society of Southern California. In January 2017 he became minister and spiritual leader of the Vedanta Society of New York, which Vivekananda founded in 1894 and which the society describes as the oldest Hindu organisation in the West.

In the academic year 2019–20 he was one of the first three Hindu monks in residence at Harvard Divinity School, in a programme funded by Vibhu and Ajit Nagral and modelled on the school's Buddhist ministry initiative. The *Gazette* reported that he took courses in Eastern and Western philosophy, including Francis X. Clooney's class on the Upanishads.

He is best known through recordings. The Vedanta Society of New York posts his classes and talks, and they are widely watched; the Omega Institute calls his videos "extremely popular globally." He has spoken at TEDx, Google and the Science and Nonduality conference, and has held public conversations with the philosopher David Chalmers, the writer Sam Harris and the non-dual teacher Rupert Spira, among others. In 2026 *The Global Indian* reported that he had been among religious and philosophical thinkers invited to talk with the AI company Anthropic about ethics and consciousness, and that he described the visit in a public talk in June 2026.

## The teaching

Sarvapriyananda teaches classical Advaita Vedanta as the Ramakrishna Order transmits it: the Upanishads read through Shankara's commentaries, together with Ramakrishna's and Vivekananda's insistence that the truth of the texts can be realised in experience. His long class series work through a text line by line, with Shankara's commentary, in the manner of a traditional teacher. A series on the Mandukya Upanishad and Gaudapada's verses on it, recorded at the Vedanta Society of New York, runs to 69 episodes in its podcast form.

**The three states.** The Mandukya Upanishad is among the texts he has taught most fully. It analyses waking, dreaming and dreamless sleep, and points to a fourth, *turiya*, which is the consciousness present through all three. Following the text, he asks students to notice what stays constant through changing experiences and to recognise that awareness as the self.

**The question of consciousness.** He has made a speciality of putting Advaita into conversation with contemporary philosophy of mind. He presents the Vedantic claim that consciousness is fundamental and self-revealing as one answer to what Western philosophers call the hard problem, the question of why physical processes should be accompanied by experience at all. Big Think's series *Dispatches from The Well* gave an episode to this theme. Asked in 2026 about artificial intelligence, he said, as reported by *The Global Indian*, "There is no clear answer to what selfhood is in the case of today's AI," and added that there is no clear answer for human beings either.

**Vedanta and Buddhism, and other faiths.** He has taught comparatively, most fully in a series later published as *Fullness & Emptiness: Vedanta and Buddhism* by the Vedanta Society of New York. On the question of this part he holds the Ramakrishna position that the world's religions are valid paths. In a 2019 lecture at Seton Hall University, reported by the student paper *The Setonian*, he argued that prejudice between religions comes from ignorance, that dialogue reveals shared values, and that people of different faiths should work together on practical charity.

His published books include *Who Am I?* (Juggernaut, 2021) and several volumes issued by the Vedanta Society of New York, among them *Mahavakya: The Essence of Vedanta* and *Reflections on Dakshinamurti Stotram*.

## Lineage

<MiniTree id="sarvapriyananda" />

He belongs to the Ramakrishna Order, and the tree connects him institutionally to Vivekananda, who founded both the order and the society he now leads. Behind that stands Shankara's Advaita, which he teaches from the commentaries. He has founded no organisation of his own and claims no personal lineage beyond the order's.
