## Life

Satyananda was born on 25 December 1923 at Almora, in the hills of what is now Uttarakhand. Wikipedia describes the family as farmers of the kshatriya caste. His name at birth is not given in the sources consulted for this entry.

In 1943, at about twenty, he went to Swami Sivananda's ashram at Rishikesh and stayed for twelve years. Sivananda initiated him into sannyasa, the vow of the renunciant monk, on 12 September 1947, on the bank of the Ganges, and named him Satyananda Saraswati. *Saraswati* is the name of one of the ten orders of monks traced to the philosopher Shankara.

In 1956 Sivananda sent him out to spread the teaching. He travelled as a wandering monk and settled on Munger, a town on the Ganges in Bihar. The movement's own history says he founded the International Yoga Fellowship Movement in 1956 and the Bihar School of Yoga at Munger in 1963. Other accounts give 1962, at Rajnandgaon, for the Fellowship and 1964 for the school.

The school became a publishing house as much as an ashram. Its manual *Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha* appeared in 1969 and became one of the most widely used handbooks of hatha yoga in English. *Yoga Nidra* followed in 1976. The Munger books presented each technique with numbered steps, cautions and the benefits claimed for it, and teachers trained there carried the system to Europe and Australia.

In 1988 he handed the running of the school to his successor, Swami Niranjanananda Saraswati, and left Munger. In September 1989 he moved to Rikhia, near Deoghar in what is now Jharkhand, and founded the ashram called Rikhiapeeth, where he lived in seclusion. He died on 5 December 2009. His followers describe his death as *mahasamadhi*, a yogi's conscious leaving of the body.

## The teaching

Satyananda taught yoga as a graded training of body, breath and mind that anyone could take up, householder or monk. From Sivananda he kept the idea that hatha, raja, karma, bhakti and jnana yoga belong together. To this he added a large body of technique he drew from tantra: mudras, bandhas (internal locks of the throat, abdomen and pelvic floor), kriyas and the awakening of kundalini, the energy that the tantric texts place at the base of the spine.

His best-known contribution is yoga nidra, "yogic sleep." The student lies on the back and is guided by a spoken script. The practice opens with a *sankalpa*, a short personal resolve, and then leads attention around the parts of the body in a fixed order, then to the breath, to paired sensations such as heavy and light, and to a series of images, before the resolve is repeated. The aim is to stay aware at the edge of sleep. The description of the stages is the usual one given for the Munger method and was not checked against the book here. Satyananda is reported to have presented the method as his own systematisation of older tantric practice, in particular of *nyasa*, the ritual placing of mantras on parts of the body.

Yoga nidra spread far beyond his movement. It is now taught in clinics and studios, and several research groups have studied its use for stress and sleep. His books put forward large health claims for many practices, which are the school's own claims.

## Questions raised

**The Mangrove Mountain ashram.** In about 1974 Satyananda sent his disciple Swami Akhandananda Saraswati to oversee the development of an ashram at Mangrove Mountain, north of Sydney. The Royal Commission into Institutional Responses to Child Sexual Abuse examined it as Case Study 21. Its public hearing ran in Sydney from 2 to 10 December 2014, with oral submissions on 29 April 2015, and its report was dated April 2016 and released on 14 September 2016. The hearing concerned how the ashram responded to allegations of child sexual abuse by Akhandananda in the 1970s and 1980s.

The Commission heard from eleven adults, ten of them women, who said they had been sexually abused as children at or in connection with the ashram. Akhandananda had been arrested in 1989 on child sexual abuse charges, convicted, and sentenced that year to two years and four months' imprisonment. According to press reports, he served about fourteen months before the High Court of Australia overturned his convictions on appeal in 1991. He died in 1997. The Commission found that no child protection policy existed at the ashram between 1975 and 1989. It concluded that the ashram's 2014 support package for survivors had probably been prepared without consulting them, and that the Bihar School of Yoga's response "did not properly prioritise the welfare of survivors" and was focused on the reputation of Satyananda Yoga. It also concluded that Satyananda, as head of the movement worldwide, had overarching authority at the Mangrove Mountain ashram.

Two witnesses alleged that Satyananda himself had sexually abused them. The case study's terms concerned the response to allegations against Akhandananda, and, according to secondary accounts of the report, the Commission made no finding on the allegations against Satyananda. The Bihar School of Yoga's 2014 submission described those allegations as out of scope and untested. Satyananda had died in 2009 and was never charged.

**Responses.** The ashram's directors apologised for the abuse of the 1970s and 1980s, and Mangrove Yoga made compensation payments to survivors. The Bihar School of Yoga first distanced itself from the Australian ashram. The Commission noted that its statement and closing submission dealt with the allegations against Akhandananda and did not refer to the alleged conduct of Satyananda. Yoga Australia, the national teachers' body, later issued its own statement acknowledging the Commission's findings.

## Lineage

Satyananda is one of the main branches of the Sivananda line, alongside Swami Vishnudevananda in the West and Chinmayananda in Vedanta teaching.

His initiation by Sivananda in 1947 is documented. His transfer of the school to Niranjanananda in 1988 is documented by the movement. Akhandananda appears on the tree because the Royal Commission records that Satyananda sent him to Australia in about 1974.
