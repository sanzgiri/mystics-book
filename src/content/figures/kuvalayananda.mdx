## Life

Jagannath Ganesh Gune was born on 30 August 1883. Kaivalyadhama's own history, the India Post stamp listing and Wikipedia name Dabhoi, in the Baroda region, as his birthplace. The institute's biographer R. K. Bodhe writes only "a village in Gujarat." His father was Ganesh Laxman Gune and his mother Saraswati, and according to Bodhe he lost both parents around the age of fourteen. He studied at the Nutan Marathi Vidyalaya in Pune, matriculated in 1903 with a Sanskrit scholarship and took his degree at Baroda College in 1910.

Between 1907 and 1910 he trained in physical culture under Rajaratna Manikrao at Baroda. Kaivalyadhama says Lokmanya Tilak's Home Rule agitation and the lectures of the young Sri Aurobindo, then teaching at Baroda, turned him toward national service. He helped found the Khandesh Education Society at Amalner and became principal of its National College in 1916. The British government withdrew recognition from the college in 1920. Bodhe adds that he corresponded with Mahatma Gandhi and advised him on yogic exercises.

He met the yogi Paramahamsa Madhavdas, who had settled at Malsar near Baroda, in 1919 by Wikipedia's and Kaivalyadhama's reckoning and at the end of 1918 by Bodhe's. Kaivalyadhama gives Madhavdas's dates as 1798 to 1921, a span that Wikipedia marks as lacking a source. Madhavdas became his teacher and encouraged him to study yoga scientifically. In 1920 and 1921 Kuvalayananda and his students began measuring the effects of yogic practices in the laboratory of the Baroda hospital, and he continued until 1923.

Kaivalyadhama, the institution, dates to 1924 at Lonavla, in the hills between Bombay and Pune. Bodhe puts the founding on Vijayadashami of that year, which he gives as 7 October. Kaivalyadhama's "Legacy" page instead says he founded it in 1917 at Amalner and set up the Lonavla research centre in 1924. In October 1924 the first issue of *Yoga Mimamsa* appeared. It was devoted to uddiyana, and the institution calls the journal the oldest scientific journal on yoga. A Bombay branch opened in 1932. The Gordhandas Seksaria College of Yoga followed in 1951. He died at Lonavla on 18 April 1966. India Post issued a commemorative stamp on 30 August 2019.

## The teaching

Kuvalayananda said he was a strict rationalist, and his aim was to test the claims of hatha yoga against measurement. The first published experiment was an X-ray study of nauli, in which the abdominal muscles are made to roll. His team also measured pressure inside the abdomen and found that it fell, which he named the "Madhavdas vacuum" after his teacher. Researchers at the institute later wrote that studies of the 1930s extended to blood pressure, postures, intragastric pressure and the air in the lungs, and they note the limits of this early work. The images of 1924 are described as cloudy.

The institute's own summary reports a result that disappointed a legend. An adept shut in an airtight pit lasted up to eighteen hours, against twelve to fourteen for people with no training, and the study found no true *bhugarbha samadhi*, the burial in the earth of yogic lore. Carbon dioxide was the main limit. Kuvalayananda wrote practical manuals, *Asanas* and *Pranayama*, and translated the *Goraksa Shatakam* and the *Vasishtha Samhita*.

His position was that experiment could renew yoga for the spiritual and the material life alike, and that inner strength and physical strength matter equally. He wrote of the institution, "I have brought up this institution out of nothing."

### Questions raised

The anthropologist Joseph Alter, in *Yoga in Modern India* (Princeton, 2004), calls him one of the chief architects of the yoga renaissance and reads his science as nationalism. In a reviewer's summary of Alter, it became "a universalist philosophy of science." Alter's reading is that a yoga defined by physiology has already given up part of its older meaning. Kaivalyadhama's own 2025 survey of its early journal acknowledges that the methods were limited. The institute's claims to be the first, or the first peer-reviewed, differ from page to page.

## Lineage

Kuvalayananda drew on a yogi near Baroda, a teacher of physical culture and the nationalist circle around Tilak and Aurobindo.

<MiniTree id="kuvalayananda" />

The tie to Madhavdas is a guru-disciple relation that Kaivalyadhama states and no independent record confirms. His debt to Manikrao is described in the institute's account of his training, and the influence of Aurobindo comes from the same source. Through Kaivalyadhama, his method passed into the yoga colleges and yoga therapy of independent India.
