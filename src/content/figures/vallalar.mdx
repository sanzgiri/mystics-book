## Life

Ramalingam was born on 5 October 1823 at Marudur, near Chidambaram, the youngest of five children of Ramayya Pillai and Chinnammai. His father died the next year. His mother took the family to Chennai around 1826, where he lived with his eldest brother Sabhapathy. The boy preferred the Kandha Kottam temple to school. He reportedly had a vision of Muruga as a child while meditating before a lamp and mirror.

He grew up in the Chennai neighbourhood of Ezhukinaru and visited the Tiruvotriyur temple, where he composed his first hymns. In 1858 he left Chennai, debated at Chidambaram, and lived nine years at Karunguzhi, near Vadalur.

Three institutions mark the Vadalur years. In 1865 he founded a fellowship, first called the Samarasa Vedha Sanmarga Sangam. In 1872 it was renamed (<span class="script" lang="ta">சமரச சுத்த சன்மார்க்க சத்திய சங்கம்</span>), the Samarasa Suddha Sanmarga Sathya Sangam. On 23 May 1867 he opened a free kitchen on land given by local villagers, serving anyone regardless of caste. On 25 January 1872, Thai Poosam day, he opened the Sathya Gnana Sabha, the Hall of True Knowledge.

On 30 January 1874, according to his followers, he went into a room at Mettukuppam, told disciples not to open it and said he would not be found there. Wikipedia reports that the government forced the door in May 1874 and found it empty. Another account says it was opened a year later. Followers believe he merged into the light, and this is a claim of faith.

## The teaching

The centre of his teaching is *jeevakarunyam*, compassion for every living being. The scholar C. Srinivasan, writing in 1968, called it his "watchword" and described absolute compassion toward all living things. In practice it meant feeding the hungry without asking their caste, refusing to kill animals for food or sacrifice, and treating cruelty as the barrier to knowing God. He taught that God is Arutperunjothi, the light of grace, and gave his followers a short mantra, *Arutperunjothi, Thanipperunkarunai* (<span class="script" lang="ta">அருட்பெருஞ்ஜோதி தனிப்பெருங்கருணை</span>), which Srinivasan translates "Supreme Grace-Light: Supreme Universal Compassion."

He called his way **Suddha Sanmarga**, the "pure true path." He placed it above six older paths including Vedanta and Siddhanta, and he rejected caste, idol worship and elaborate ritual. The Sathya Gnana Sabha reflects this. It is an octagonal hall with no image. A sanctum is hidden behind seven curtains, which are drawn back only on Thai Poosam, and worshippers face a lamp's flame as a symbol of the Divine. Its lamp is kept lit at all times.

His most unusual claim is about the body. Srinivasan summarises the aim as the "conquest of death" through grace, a progressive transformation of the body through stages he named the golden body, the body of grace and the body of bliss. Followers read the disappearance story in this light.

## Lineage

<MiniTree id="vallalar" />

His hymns belong to the Tamil Shaiva tradition that runs back to the Nayanars. No human guru is named in the sources consulted. His influence runs forward. The founder Vethathiri Maharishi is said by one source to have drawn inspiration from him, which is a connection by testimony, since the two never met.

## Critiques and questions

**The naming dispute.** The traditionalist scholar Arumuga Navalar rejected Vallalar's hymns, calling them *Marutpa*, poems of delusion, on the argument that the title *Tiruvarutpa* belonged to ancient Shaiva works. The feud, called the Arutpa-Marutpa dispute, ran for several decades and included a lawsuit. The outcome is not given in the sources consulted here.

**The disappearance.** The accounts consulted differ on when the room was opened, and the claim that he merged into light rests on his followers' testimony.
