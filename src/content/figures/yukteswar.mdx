## Life

Priya Nath Karar was born on 10 May 1855 in Serampore, a river town on the Hooghly about twelve miles from Calcutta. His parents were Kshetranath and Kadambini Karar. He married and had a daughter. In the words Yogananda records, "After my wife died, I joined the Swami Order." The Ananda movement dates her death to 1895. His father left him the two-storey family house in Serampore, which became his hermitage.

He became a disciple of Lahiri Mahasaya in Benares in 1884, according to Wikipedia and the Ananda movement, and received initiation into Kriya Yoga. Lahiri Mahasaya was a householder who taught breathing and meditation techniques to married people as well as monks. The Kriya Yoga Institute says Lahiri called his new student "Priya," the loved one.

In January 1894, by Yukteswar's own account as Yogananda reports it, he went to the Kumbha Mela at Allahabad and met Mahavatar Babaji, the deathless master the Kriya lineage says taught Lahiri. Babaji is known only through disciples' accounts. In this telling he asked Yukteswar to write "a short book on the underlying basic unity between the Christian and Hindu scriptures," and conferred the title *swami*. Yukteswar wrote the book that year. Sources disagree about when he was formally ordained. Ananda gives 1906, when he took the name Sri Yukteswar Giri. Wikipedia says a mahant at Bodh Gaya ordained him and gives no year. Giri is one of the ten branches of the Swami order, and Yogananda later joined the same branch.

In 1903 he founded a second hermitage at Puri near the sea. The Kriya Yoga Institute dates it to 22 March. Disciples lived and studied at both places.

In Benares around 1910, a young man named Mukunda Lal Ghosh saw him in a lane and knelt at his feet. Yogananda's *Autobiography of a Yogi* describes Yukteswar at that time as "about fifty-five." Mukunda trained with him for nearly ten years and became Swami Yogananda in 1914. Yukteswar sent him to the West in 1920. In Yogananda's account the teacher was reserved and severe. He called him "hypercritical of his disciples" but also said he gave him one instruction early: "Someday you will go to the West." Yukteswar died at Puri on 9 March 1936, during Yogananda's return visit to India after fifteen years in America. Yogananda says he appeared to him afterwards in a hotel in Bombay. That is a claim of the movement's biography.

## The teaching

*The Holy Science* has four sections: the Gospel, the Goal, the Procedure and the Revelation. The Sanskrit title means roughly the vision of liberation. The book sets passages of the Bible, above all the Book of Revelation, beside Upanishadic and yogic texts, and says that all scriptures admit one goal. Yukteswar presents liberation as a matter of knowledge and practice, and the practice behind the book is Kriya Yoga.

The part that made the book known is its account of time. Hindu tradition divides time into four ages, or *yugas*, and gives the present age, Kali Yuga, 432,000 years from 3102 BCE. Yukteswar held that this figure came from errors in the almanacs. He said the real cycle is 24,000 years, with a 12,000-year descending half and a 12,000-year ascending half. He gave a cause. The Sun, he wrote, "takes some star for its dual and revolves round it in about 24,000 years," and that motion accounts for the slow drift of the equinoxes.

The numbers follow. In his scheme the lowest point of the cycle fell around the year 500, and Kali Yuga lasted until about 1700. Since then the world has been in an ascending Dwapara Yuga, an age of electricity and atomic energy, which lasts 2,400 years. The next age begins around 4100. Yukteswar read modern science as a sign that human capacities were rising, not falling.

He also taught astrology as a practical tool. Yogananda records that he advised a metal armlet for a stubborn illness and said the stars have no goodwill or ill will of their own and that a birth chart is meant to strengthen the will.

### Questions raised

Astronomers explain the precession of the equinoxes by the pull of the Sun and Moon on the Earth's equatorial bulge, with a period of about 25,771 years, and this account has no use for a companion star. The Binary Research Institute, a small body, is investigating Yukteswar's version. Wikipedia notes that most Indian pundits hold that the present age is still Kali Yuga. Almost everything known about his life comes from the Kriya lineage, chiefly Yogananda, and independent records are few.

## Lineage

Yukteswar stands between Lahiri Mahasaya and Yogananda, and so connects the Bengal householder Kriya tradition to its largest international branch.

<MiniTree id="yukteswar" />

The edge from Lahiri to Yukteswar rests on the testimony of the lineage and on Yogananda. The edge from Babaji is a claim made by Yukteswar himself and is shown as hagiographic. The Kriya Yoga Institute names Paramahamsa Hariharananda as another disciple. Yogananda is the one whose work reached large audiences, and through him Yukteswar became known outside Bengal.
