## Life

Vijay Kumar was born on 7 March 1949 in Natham, a village in what is now Vellore district of Tamil Nadu. His father, Varadarajulu, worked in the accounts department of the Indian Railways, and the family moved to Madras in 1955. He went to Don Bosco School and took a degree in mathematics at D.G. Vaishnav College, then worked as a clerk at the Life Insurance Corporation of India. On 9 June 1977 he married Padmavathi, born in 1954 in Sangam, Andhra Pradesh.

In 1984 the couple opened Jeevashram, a residential school in Chittoor district of Andhra Pradesh, meant as an alternative to ordinary schooling. According to the movement's own history, in 1989 some pupils reported mystical experiences, and the couple began offering courses to the local public. Around then Vijay Kumar took the name Kalki, after the tenth and final avatar of Vishnu, who in Hindu tradition comes at the end of the present dark age. Followers called him Sri Bhagavan and his wife Sri Amma, and together Amma Bhagavan. In the early years the work was called the Kalki movement.

The school closed in 1994 and its campus became Satyaloka, a centre for courses. Some graduates formed the Order of Dasas, the movement's monastic workers. In 1999 land was bought near Varadaiahpalem, in what is now Tirupati district, and Oneness University opened there in 2000. From 2004 it ran a 21-day course for foreigners, and from that year trainers were authorised to give the blessing, which spread to groups abroad, among them one in Taipei by 2005. A large white Oneness Temple opened on the campus on 22 April 2008. The movement announced 21 December 2012 as the start of a golden age, and said in 2013 that the founders' mission was complete.

In 2017, by most accounts, the couple handed the organisation to their son, N.K.V. Krishna, and his wife, Preetha Krishna. Known to followers as Krishnaji and Preethaji, they opened Ekam, a meditation hall on the Varadaiahpalem site, in January 2018. Kalki Bhagavan died on 29 September 2026 at a private hospital in Chennai, where he had been treated for what the press described as age-related complications. He was 77. His body was taken to Ekam Kshetra at Battalavallam, on the Varadaiahpalem campus, for devotees to pay their respects, and he was cremated there in early October with police honours ordered by the Andhra Pradesh government.

## The teaching

Kalki Bhagavan taught that human suffering comes from the sense of being a separate self, set against everyone and everything else, and that this sense drives conflict in families and between nations. Enlightenment, in his account, is the ending of that separateness. He described it as a change in the brain as much as in the mind, a neurobiological event that could be set off from outside.

The means was the deeksha, Sanskrit for initiation, which the movement in the West called the Oneness Blessing. A giver places the hands on the crown of the receiver's head for a short time; the movement also said it could be given by intention or through the eyes. The American teacher Arjuna Ardagh, who wrote a popular book on the movement, described it in 2008 as "a powerful transmission of energy, which rapidly accelerates the evolution of consciousness." A Taiwanese organiser told the *Taipei Times* in 2005, "We do not transfer enlightenment. We transfer an initiation into an enlightenment process." Some supporters, as the *Taipei Times* reported, offered a brain explanation, that the blessing quiets the parietal lobes and stimulates the frontal lobes; the movement gave no controlled studies for this. Followers have reported relief from illnesses they ascribed to the blessing.

The deeksha required no conversion and no new path, and the movement called it compatible with any religion. He also taught that once a critical mass of people had received it, the change would spread to the rest of humanity. The movement's line on his own status shifted. Early followers worshipped him as Kalki, Vishnu's avatar, and *Open* reported in 2019 that devotees regarded members of his family as minor gods. Later he said that "Kalki is not one person but a collective awakening," and the Oneness Temple's sanctum held a golden orb for meditation on each person's own idea of the divine.

## Lineage

<MiniTree id="kalki-bhagavan" />

The couple claimed no human guru. According to Wikipedia's biography, a childhood friend, R. Sankar, introduced Vijay Kumar to the philosophy of **J. Krishnamurti**, and a former disciple interviewed by *Open* in 2019 judged the movement against Krishnamurti's teachings. The movement's own timeline does not mention him, and the extent of the influence is thinly documented. The succession to their son and daughter-in-law in 2017 is documented, and the couple's divinity is a devotional claim.
