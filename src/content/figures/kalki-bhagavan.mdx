## Life

Vijay Kumar was born on 7 March 1949 in Natham, a village in what is now Vellore district of Tamil Nadu. His father, Varadarajulu, worked in the accounts department of the Indian Railways, and the family moved to Madras in 1955. He went to Don Bosco School and took a degree in mathematics at D.G. Vaishnav College, then worked as a clerk at the Life Insurance Corporation of India. On 9 June 1977 he married Padmavathi, born in 1954 in Sangam, Andhra Pradesh.

In 1984 the couple opened Jeevashram, a residential school in Chittoor district of Andhra Pradesh, meant as an alternative to ordinary schooling. According to the movement's own history, in 1989 some pupils reported mystical experiences, and the couple began offering courses to the local public. Around then Vijay Kumar took the name Kalki, after the tenth and final avatar of Vishnu, who in Hindu tradition comes at the end of the present dark age. Followers called him Sri Bhagavan and his wife Sri Amma, and together Amma Bhagavan. In the early years the work was called the Kalki movement.

The school closed in 1994 and its campus became Satyaloka, a centre for courses. Some graduates formed the Order of Dasas, the movement's monastic workers. In 1999 land was bought near Varadaiahpalem, in what is now Tirupati district, and Oneness University opened there in 2000. From 2004 it ran a 21-day course for foreigners, and from that year trainers were authorised to give the blessing, which spread to groups abroad, among them one in Taipei by 2005. A large white Oneness Temple opened on the campus on 22 April 2008. The movement announced 21 December 2012 as the start of a golden age, and said in 2013 that the founders' mission was complete.

In 2017, by most accounts, the couple handed the organisation and its business interests to their son, N.K.V. Krishna, and his wife, Preetha Krishna. Known to followers as Krishnaji and Preethaji, they opened Ekam, a meditation hall on the Varadaiahpalem site, in January 2018. Kalki Bhagavan died on 29 September 2026 at a private hospital in Chennai, where he had been treated for what the press described as age-related complications. He was 77. His body was taken to the Ekam campus at Battalavallam for devotees to pay their respects.

## The teaching

Kalki Bhagavan taught that human suffering comes from the sense of being a separate self, set against everyone and everything else, and that this sense drives conflict in families and between nations. Enlightenment, in his account, is the ending of that separateness. He described it as a change in the brain as much as in the mind, a neurobiological event that could be set off from outside.

The means was the deeksha, Sanskrit for initiation, which the movement in the West called the Oneness Blessing. A giver places the hands on the crown of the receiver's head for a short time; the movement also said it could be given by intention or through the eyes. The American teacher Arjuna Ardagh, who wrote a popular book on the movement, described it in 2008 as "a powerful transmission of energy, which rapidly accelerates the evolution of consciousness." A Taiwanese organiser told the *Taipei Times* in 2005, "We do not transfer enlightenment. We transfer an initiation into an enlightenment process." Some supporters offered a brain explanation, that the blessing quiets the parietal lobes and stimulates the frontal lobes; the movement gave no controlled studies for this. Followers have reported relief from illnesses they ascribed to the blessing.

The deeksha required no conversion and no new path, and the movement said it was compatible with any religion. Kalki Bhagavan also taught that once a critical mass of people had received it, the change would spread to the rest of humanity. On his own status the movement shifted. Early followers worshipped him as Kalki, Vishnu's avatar, and *Open* reported in 2019 that devotees regarded members of his family as minor gods. Later he said that "Kalki is not one person but a collective awakening," and the Oneness Temple's sanctum held a golden orb for meditation on each person's own idea of the divine.

The couple claimed no human guru. A former disciple interviewed by *Open* in 2019 judged the movement against the teachings of **J. Krishnamurti**, which suggests his ideas were part of its early background, but the link is thinly documented. The succession to their son and daughter-in-law in 2017 is documented, and the couple's divinity is a devotional claim.

## Critiques and questions

**Money and the inner circle.** Former members interviewed by *Open* magazine in 2019 said darshan of Bhagavan came to cost thousands of rupees, that monks were set donation targets, and that cash and jewellery gifts were not always receipted. One former dasa said "the organisation was growing so big that the original purpose of spirituality was lost." Others interviewed in the same piece said the couple lived modestly. In 2002 a public interest petition seeking a tax inquiry into the Kalki trust was dismissed by the Madras High Court and later by the Supreme Court.

**The income tax searches, 2019.** On Wednesday, 16 October 2019, the Income Tax Department began searches at about forty premises of the group's trusts and companies in Varadaiahpalem, Chennai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad, including ashrams in Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh. The department did not name the guru. Its statement said the group "has been regularly suppressing its receipts at its various centres or ashrams" and put a preliminary estimate of unaccounted cash receipts at Rs 409 crore from 2014–15 onward. It said the group took cash from property buyers above the recorded price and did not account for some foreign receipts from overseas course fees, which passed through companies abroad. Press reports put the seizures at about Rs 44 crore in Indian cash, about Rs 18 crore in US dollars, 88 kilograms of gold and 1,271 carats of diamonds, around Rs 93 crore in all, and quoted unnamed officials estimating evasion at more than Rs 500 crore. *The Week* reported that the Enforcement Directorate had registered a foreign exchange case against Kalki Bhagavan and Krishna. Krishna was questioned by tax officials in Chennai. The reports available do not record any criminal charge or court finding against Kalki Bhagavan arising from the searches.

**The response.** When reports said the couple had fled India, they released a video. "Firstly, we have not fled the country nor have we gone anywhere," Kalki Bhagavan said. He said they were in good health, were living at Nemam, their ashram in Tiruvallur district, and that activities at Nemam, Satyaloka and Ekam were continuing as usual. The ashram's manager told reporters the organisation had nothing to say to the media.
