## Life

He was born Dungar Patel on 31 January 1865 in Mahelav, a village in central Gujarat, the youngest of five children. In 1881 he met the monk Vignananand and decided to become a swami. Accounts of his initiation differ. A BAPS biography and the scholar Raymond Williams give 29 November 1882 at Vadtal, at the hands of the Vadtal acharya Viharilalji Maharaj, with the name Yagnapurushdas. The BAPS website says he was initiated soon after reaching the Surat temple at about sixteen. The title *Shastri* was added later for his learning in Sanskrit scripture, and followers call him Shastriji Maharaj.

In Surat, around 1883, he met Bhagatji Maharaj (1829–1897 or 1898), a tailor by caste who taught that the Swaminarayan sect's senior monk Gunatitanand Swami was the earthly form of Akshar, God's eternal abode and servant. Bhagatji had himself been excommunicated for saying so, then readmitted after nearly three years, though some argue he was never formally reinstated. The young monk took him as his guru, a decision some at the temple opposed because of Bhagatji's caste. By BAPS's account he cited the Vachanamrut to argue that spiritual standing outweighs social rank.

After Bhagatji's death, Yagnapurushdas pressed the view within the Vadtal diocese and proposed a temple that would enshrine Swaminarayan together with Gunatitanand Swami. The Vadtal leadership, who regarded the Akshar teaching as unwarranted, resisted. He left in 1905 with five monks and a small group of devotees. BAPS biographies, followed by one scholarly study, say Vadtal monks tried to poison him on 8 November 1905 and made a second attempt the next day. Williams records that a hastily called meeting of Vadtal monks expelled him, and his followers dispute that expulsion's validity. In a later suit by the Vadtal acharya over BAPS monks staying at the Ishnav temple, a district judge ruled in 1935, according to Williams, that the 1906 expulsion proceedings had breached natural justice, but also held that his monks had no right to stay or preach at that temple.

In 1907 he installed images of Swaminarayan and Gunatitanand Swami in a new temple at Bochasan; a BAPS biography gives 5 June. BAPS counts this as its founding. Similar temples followed at Sarangpur, Gondal, Atladra and Gadhada. In 1910, at Rajkot, he met a young novice who became Yogiji Maharaj (1892–1971); Yogiji took a swami's vows in 1911 and joined him. BAPS was registered as a charitable trust in 1947. On 21 May 1950 he appointed Narayanswarupdas, aged 28 and later called Pramukh Swami, as president, and he died on 10 May 1951.

## The teaching

The teaching, called Akshar-Purushottam Upasana or Darshan, is a reading of the Swaminarayan *Vachanamrut*. Five realities are eternal and distinct: the individual soul (*jiva*), the lesser divinities who administer worlds (*ishvara*), the material cause of the world (*maya*), Aksharbrahman and Parabrahman. Parabrahman, also called Purushottam, is God, and BAPS holds that he appeared as Swaminarayan (1781–1830). Aksharbrahman is a second, subordinate reality that exists in four forms. It is God's abode, his ideal devotee, an all-pervading consciousness, and the guru who stays on earth.

The last of these gives the movement its practical shape. Liberation comes through attachment to an Aksharbrahman guru, and BAPS names Gunatitanand Swami (1784 or 1785 to 1867) as the first and Bhagatji Maharaj, Shastriji Maharaj, Yogiji Maharaj, Pramukh Swami Maharaj and the present guru Mahant Swami Maharaj as successors. The original Swaminarayan dioceses, Vadtal and Ahmedabad, regard Akshar as the abode of God. Their leadership rejects the BAPS identification of Gunatitanand Swami and his successors with Akshar, though some groups within both dioceses still honour Gunatitanand in that way.

The theology was later given a Sanskrit commentary on the Upanishads, Gita and Brahma Sutras, the *Swaminarayan Bhashyam* (2007) by Bhadreshdas Swami. In July 2017 a Varanasi council of scholars, the Kashi Vidvat Parishad, stated that it was appropriate to call this view a distinct Vedantic school, separate from Advaita and Vishishtadvaita. Most sources for that recognition come from BAPS.

## Lineage

BAPS traces its gurus from Swaminarayan through Gunatitanand Swami, Bhagatji Maharaj, Shastriji Maharaj, Yogiji Maharaj, Pramukh Swami Maharaj (1921–2016) and Mahant Swami Maharaj. Yogiji Maharaj led after 1951, consecrated a temple at Mombasa on his 1955 East Africa tour, opened the first BAPS temple in London in 1970, and died in Mumbai on 23 January 1971. Pramukh Swami then led for forty-five years. On 20 July 2012 he named Mahant Swami as his successor, and Mahant Swami became guru when Pramukh Swami died on 13 August 2016.

Swaminarayan's life and Gunatitanand Swami's discipleship are documented in the movement's early texts. The claim that Swaminarayan named Gunatitanand his spiritual successor is the BAPS position, and the Vadtal and Ahmedabad dioceses reject it. Later transmission from Bhagatji to Shastriji and onward is documented in BAPS biographies.
