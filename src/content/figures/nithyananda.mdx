## Life

Nithyananda was born Arunachalam Rajasekaran in Tiruvannamalai, the temple town at the foot of the hill Arunachala, in Tamil Nadu. Most accounts give his birth date as 1 January 1978; some give 1977. His own organisation says he trained as a mechanical engineer and that he had a spiritual awakening as a boy. *Quartz* reported that he joined the Ramakrishna Math in Chennai in 1995, under the influence of his grandfather.

In 2003 he founded the Nithyananda Dhyanapeetam, an ashram at Bidadi on the Bengaluru–Mysuru road, and in 2006 the Life Bliss Foundation in California. His discourses, given in Tamil and English and spread on television and YouTube, drew a large following among Tamils at home and abroad, and his centres multiplied in India and the United States. In 2012 he was named the 293rd head of the Madurai Aadheenam, an old Shaiva monastery, by its elderly pontiff. The appointment was withdrawn the same year after opposition from the Tamil Nadu government, other Shaiva monasteries and the monastery's own followers. In 2013 he received the title Mahamandaleshwar.

In March 2010 a video broadcast by Sun TV and other channels appeared to show him in a hotel room with the actress Ranjitha. A criminal case followed, described below, and he was arrested in Himachal Pradesh in April 2010 and released on bail in June. In November 2019, after a police case in Gujarat named him, the Gujarat police said he had left the country. In December 2019 he announced Kailasa, which he and his followers describe as a sovereign state for Hindus. His whereabouts are not publicly known. On 1 April 2025, after a relative was reported to have said he had died, Kailasa issued a statement that he was "alive, safe, and active."

## The teaching

Nithyananda presents himself as an enlightened master and, in his organisation's words, as an avatar, a living incarnation of what it calls superconsciousness. He draws on Shaiva tradition and the Agamas, the ritual scriptures of Shiva worship, and his broadcast programmes promise to awaken *Shivoham*, the realisation "I am Shiva." Later he took the name Nithyananda Paramashivam, and Kailasa's flag shows Paramashiva, the supreme Shiva, with the bull Nandi and Nithyananda seated below.

His method combines meditation programmes, such as the residential Inner Awakening course and daily satsangs streamed online, with temple ritual and claims of powers. He and his organisation have said he can heal illness, open a person's third eye, and make animals speak. During the 2010 controversy he played these claims down. "I teach only meditation, yogic kriyas," he said in an interview, describing his healing as help for psychosomatic disorders.

Kailasa extends the teaching into politics. Its representatives describe it as a revival of "the enlightened Hindu civilisation," a borderless nation for persecuted Hindus run through non-profit bodies, with its own passport, flag and a Reserve Bank of Kailasa, launched in August 2020.

## Lineage

<MiniTree id="nithyananda" />

Nithyananda names no single human guru whose line he continues. His reported time at the **Ramakrishna Math** rests on his organisation's account. His tie to **Tamil Shaivism** is documented through the brief Madurai Aadheenam appointment of 2012 and the Agamic worship of his temples.

## Critiques and questions

**The Karnataka case, 2010 to the present.** After the video was broadcast, a former driver and disciple, Kuruppan Lenin, complained to the Chennai police. The case was moved to the Ramanagara police in Karnataka and registered under sections of the Indian Penal Code covering rape, unnatural sex, cheating, criminal intimidation, conspiracy and outraging religious feelings. A former disciple accused him of raping her over about five years at the ashram. He was arrested in Solan district, Himachal Pradesh, in April 2010, and the Karnataka High Court granted him bail on 11 June 2010 on condition that he surrender his passport and give no public discourses. In 2017 a forensic laboratory report was reported to have found that the man in the video was Nithyananda; he and Ranjitha had said in 2010 that the video was morphed. In June 2018 the Third Additional District and Sessions Court, Ramanagara, framed charges against him and five co-accused, who pleaded not guilty. *The News Minute* reported that during the proceedings he first said he was impotent and later that the relationship had been consensual. On 5 February 2020 the High Court cancelled his bail, finding that he had "misled the trial court into believing that he was working towards propagating Hinduism" while his whereabouts were unknown. The trial court has issued non-bailable warrants; in 2022 it noted that the trial had stalled for about three years in his absence. He has not been convicted, and the case is pending.

**The Gujarat case, 2019.** In November 2019 Janardhana Sharma, a former follower, went to the Gujarat High Court seeking the return of his daughters from the movement's ashram in Ahmedabad, the Yogini Sarvajnapeetham. The Ahmedabad rural police registered an FIR naming Nithyananda for kidnapping, wrongful confinement, causing hurt and offences under the child labour law, after children said they had been made to work and collect donations. Two women who managed the ashram were arrested. Sharma's two adult daughters, aged 21 and 18, said in videos that they were staying of their own will and accused their father of using them against the guru. The reports available do not record charges being filed against Nithyananda himself or a trial in this case.

**Kailasa's recognition.** No government or international body recognises Kailasa. In December 2019 Ecuador's embassy "categorically" denied reports that it had given him asylum, and India's foreign ministry said he had left the country without a valid passport. On 22 and 24 February 2023, a woman calling herself Kailasa's permanent ambassador, Vijayapriya Nithyananda, spoke at open sessions of two UN committees in Geneva, including the Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, and said Nithyananda was persecuted. The UN human rights office said one submission was "irrelevant to the topic of discussion" and the other "tangential to the topic at hand," and would not be considered. She then said she meant persecution by anti-Hindu elements and that Kailasa "respects India as its Gurupeedam." Newark, New Jersey, which had signed a sister-city agreement with Kailasa in January 2023, cancelled it within a week, saying that "Based on deception, the (sister-city) ceremony was groundless and void." In March 2025 Bolivia deported twenty people linked to Kailasa who had signed thousand-year land leases with Indigenous groups, and a minister said the government would never endorse "these types of hidden contracts."

**The movement's response.** Nithyananda and his followers deny the allegations and describe the cases as persecution of a Hindu revivalist by hostile forces. Kailasa's statement of April 2025 condemned what it called a "malicious smear campaign."
