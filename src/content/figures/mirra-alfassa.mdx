## Life

Mirra Alfassa was born in Paris on 21 February 1878 to Mathilde Ismalun, an Egyptian Jew, and Maurice Alfassa, a Turkish Jew from Edirne. The ashram's biographical essay calls her parents materialists. Wikipedia reports that at thirteen or fourteen she saw a luminous figure she took to be Krishna, and that she kept such experiences to herself. She joined the Académie Julian to study painting in 1893, married the painter Henri Morisset in 1897, had a son, André, in 1898, and showed pictures at the Salon d'Automne between 1903 and 1905. Before she met any Indian teacher she read Vivekananda's *Raja Yoga* and the *Bhagavad Gita*, which she found helped her make sense of her experiences.

She met Max Théon, whose Cosmic Movement taught an occult discipline, in Paris in 1905, and stayed with him and his wife at Tlemcen in Algeria in the summers of 1906 and 1907. She later described meeting there the "Principle" of the human form, which Aurobindo, according to the ashram essay, called the prototype of the supramental form. She and Morisset were divorced in 1908, and in 1911 she married Paul Richard. The essay says she had seen Aurobindo in visions about ten years before they met. She reached Pondicherry with Richard on 29 March 1914 and recognised him as the figure of those visions. The two sources agree that after the war began she left for France and then spent about four years in Japan. She came back to Pondicherry in April 1920 and moved into Aurobindo's house in November of that year.

On 24 November 1926, which the ashram counts as the descent of the Overmind into matter, Aurobindo withdrew from dealing with people and put her in charge of the disciples and of the ashram's outer life. Wikipedia dates his declaration of her sole charge to 1930. A school for about twenty children began inside the ashram on 2 December 1943. Aurobindo died on 5 December 1950, and she carried the work on alone, and on 6 January 1952 she inaugurated the Sri Aurobindo International University Centre, which a convention had resolved to found in 1951. By her own account the supramental light, force and consciousness descended upon the earth on 29 February 1956, and in a message of 24 April 1956 she wrote <q>The manifestation of the Supramental upon earth is no more a promise but a living fact, a reality.</q> From 1958 she turned to what her ashram calls the yoga of the cells, and from the early 1960s she withdrew from most outside activity.

In 1964 the Sri Aurobindo Society, with her as executive president, resolved to found a city. Auroville was inaugurated on 28 February 1968 on a plateau about ten kilometres north of Pondicherry, with delegates from 124 countries placing soil from their homelands in a marble urn. She spoke from her room by radio and read her four-point Charter in French. The first point says that Auroville belongs to no one in particular but to humanity as a whole. She died on 17 November 1973, aged ninety-five, and was buried beside Aurobindo three days later.

## Why this figure appears here

Part 4 asks whether spirit is evolving. Aurobindo supplied the philosophy, in which the Supermind descends into matter and life moves toward a new species. The Mother tried to make that claim happen in a body and in a community. Wikipedia notes that the two called their approach Integral Yoga, and that it replaced monastic withdrawal with practice inside ordinary life. Her ashram essay describes four aspects of her nature that Aurobindo named, Wisdom, Power, Harmony and Perfection in works, and treats her as the executive side of the partnership.

She drew on several sources. The Gita and Vivekananda's *Raja Yoga* gave her a vocabulary for experiences she already had, and Théon's school supplied a method and the idea of a prototype form. Aurobindo gave her the framework of a world in evolution toward a divine life.

Three things look new. From 1958 she claimed that the cells of the body hold a consciousness that can be changed, and her conversations on this with the disciple Satprem were collected as *Mother's Agenda*. The 1943 school and the 1952 university centre were set up on the premise that children could be prepared for supramental work. Auroville was an experiment in which strangers from many nations were to live out the Charter's phrase about a living embodiment of human unity. The ashram essay calls the township a laboratory of new evolution. Wikipedia counts 2,814 residents from 54 countries in January 2018.

On the tree she hangs from Aurobindo as his collaborator, an edge that is documented, and she heads the institutions that came after him. Sri Chinmoy, who grew up in the ashram from 1944, is documented as living under her direction there. Most claims about the cell work and the supramental result rest on the ashram's own accounts, which are devotional in kind and cannot be checked from outside.
