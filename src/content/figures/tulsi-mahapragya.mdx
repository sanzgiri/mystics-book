## Life

Acharya Tulsi was born on 20 October 1914 in Ladnun, in Rajasthan, to Vadana and Jhumarmal Khater. He was initiated as a Terapanthi monk on 5 December 1925, aged eleven. On 26 August 1936 at Gangapur, his teacher Acharya Kalugani named him successor, which made him the ninth acharya of the Terapanth order.

The Terapanth is a Shvetambara Jain order. Wikipedia's article on its founder, Acharya Bhikshu (1726–1803), says he left the Sthanakvasi group of Acharya Raghunath after concluding that monastic discipline had slipped, and that the name refers to the number thirteen: thirteen monks, thirteen followers and thirteen rules. The page gives no founding year.

Nathmal, the future Mahapragya, was born on 14 June 1920 in the village of Tamkor. According to the Jainology obituary, his father died within weeks of his birth and the village had no school. Acharya Kalugani initiated him on 29 January 1931, aged ten, and he became a disciple of Tulsi. In 1955 Tulsi named him chief secretary of the order. Tulsi gave him the name Mahapragya on 12 November 1978 and made him *yuvacharya*, the successor designate, on 4 February 1979.

On 18 February 1994 Tulsi gave up the office, and Mahapragya was formally installed as tenth acharya in Delhi on 5 February 1995. Tulsi died on 23 June 1997. Mahapragya led the order until his death on 9 May 2010; his own chosen successor, Mahashraman, became eleventh acharya.

Both were walking monks. Wikipedia's article on Tulsi says he walked more than 70,000 kilometres in his lifetime. Mahapragya led an Ahimsa Yatra, a foot pilgrimage for nonviolence, from 5 December 2001 to 4 January 2009, which Wikipedia says covered 87 districts and more than 2,400 villages, towns and cities.

## The teaching

Jain monks take five great vows (*mahavrata*): nonviolence, truth, not stealing, celibacy and non-possession. Tulsi launched the Anuvrat movement in 1949, at Sardarshahar according to the Jainology obituary of Mahapragya. It offered the same five principles in reduced form (*anuvrata*, "small vows") to lay people of any religion. Wikipedia says the movement held that dharma should bring happiness in this life and not only in later ones. Mahapragya later led the movement and, by the Jainology account, carried it abroad.

The second programme is *preksha dhyana*, "meditation of perception." Jainology says Tulsi asked Mahapragya to find out why meditation had faded from Jainism after Mahavira's time, and that Mahapragya spent over twenty years studying the Jain scriptures. Wikipedia's article on Mahapragya says he formulated the system in 1975 and that training camps began in 1977. Wikipedia's article on preksha lists eight parts: relaxation of the body with awareness (*kayotsarga*), an internal journey of attention along the spine, perception of breath, perception of the body, perception of psychic centres, perception of psychic colours (*leshya*), auto-suggestion and contemplation. The article says Mahapragya mapped the psychic centres onto the endocrine system. It also derives *preksha* from the root *iksa*, "to see," and defines it as perceiving carefully and profoundly, without liking or disliking what is seen.

Mahapragya also conceived a school programme, *jeevan vigyan* ("science of living"), on 28 December 1979 at Ladnun, according to Wikipedia. Of religion he said, "Religion is beyond rituals."

## Lineage

Tulsi stood in the Terapanth line founded by Acharya Bhikshu, and Kalugani named him successor. Mahapragya stood in the same line after him. On the tree the node hangs from the Jain tradition, and Bhikshu is added as a bridge.
