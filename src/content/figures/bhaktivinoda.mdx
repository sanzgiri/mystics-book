## Life

Kedarnath Datta was born on 2 September 1838 in the village of Ula, now called Birnagar, in Nadia district, a few hours from Navadvip, the town Bengalis associate with Chaitanya. He grew up in a Kayastha family, was educated in Krishnanagar and Calcutta, and was taught English, Persian and Sanskrit. As a young man he read widely in Western writing, including Unitarian and Transcendentalist authors, and was for a time drawn to the Brahmo Samaj.

He then entered the British judicial service as a deputy magistrate, an office he held from about 1861 until his retirement in October 1894. Postings took him through Bengal and Orissa, with long stays at Puri, where he helped oversee the Jagannath temple and ran daily Bhagavata readings. The work of a colonial official left him time for writing, and from the late 1870s he published steadily.

His first major book, *Krishna-samhita* (1879), defended Krishna against the charge, common among educated Indians and missionaries, that the Krishna of the Puranas was morally indefensible. He is reported to have sent copies to Western scholars, among them Reinhold Rost, who wrote back with praise in 1880. In 1880 he took formal initiation from Bipin Bihari Goswami, a hereditary guru of the Baghnapara family. In January 1886 the Baghnapara goswamis gave him the title Bhaktivinoda, "one who delights in devotion." The Gaudiya Math tradition holds that his instructing guru was the ascetic Jagannath Das Babaji of Vrindavan, a claim other descendants of his family do not accept.

From 1881 he edited *Sajjana-toshani*, a Bengali monthly that ran until 1909. He published the *Chaitanya-siksamrita* (1886), translated the Bhagavad Gita into Bengali with a medieval commentary, and in 1896 issued *Jaiva Dharma*, a novel in which a Vaishnava teacher, a sceptical Brahmin and several seekers argue their way through the theology. That year he also produced an English introduction to Chaitanya's life and teaching, which reached libraries in Britain and North America.

In the late 1880s he concluded that Chaitanya's birthplace lay in a village then called Ballaldighi, which he identified with Mayapur, on the opposite bank of the Ganges from the town of Navadvip. Accounts give 1887 for the identification, and an organisation for the sacred precinct, the Navadvipa Dhama Pracarini Sabha, followed his lecture of February 1891. He also founded Nama-hatta, a network of preaching centres whose name means "marketplace of the name." Sources date its start to 1886 or 1890. Its meetings offered kirtan, lectures and shared food to householders, and one account counts over five hundred centres in Bengal by the early twentieth century. He took the dress of a renunciant in 1908 and died in 1914.

## The teaching

Bhaktivinoda taught the theology Chaitanya (1486–1534) and his Vrindavan disciples had set down. Krishna is the supreme person, the individual soul is a tiny part of him, and the relation between them is described as *achintya bhedabheda*, "inconceivable oneness and difference." Liberation is a loving service to Krishna that continues after release, and the best method in the present age is the chanting of the holy name.

His emphasis lay in how this was argued and spread. He answered the objections of English-educated readers in their own terms, drawing the case for personal devotion out of reason and ethics as well as scripture. He also wrote devotional songs in Bengali, some of which are still sung in Gaudiya temples. In *Jaiva Dharma* the doctrine arrives through dialogue and story, and in Nama-hatta it was meant for people with jobs and families, outside the hereditary guru households and the ascetic orders.

## Lineage

Bhaktivinoda stands in the Gaudiya line from Chaitanya through the Goswamis. His sons carried the work forward, above all Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, who founded the Gaudiya Math, and through him A. C. Bhaktivedanta Prabhupada, who founded ISKCON.

<MiniTree id="bhaktivinoda" />

The family link to Bhaktisiddhanta is documented. Bhaktivinoda's initiation under Bipin Bihari Goswami is also documented. The role of Jagannath Das Babaji as his instructing guru rests on the Gaudiya Math's own tradition, and descendants of his son Lalita Prasad name Bipin Bihari Goswami instead. The Mayapur site remains disputed, and Navadvip residents have rejected it since the 1880s. A 2023 article by Ferdinando Sardella surveys the competing evidence.
