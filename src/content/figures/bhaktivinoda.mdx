## Life

Kedarnath Datta was born on 2 September 1838 in the village of Ula, now called Birnagar, in Nadia district, a few hours from Navadvip, the town Bengalis associate with Chaitanya. Wikipedia names his parents as Ananda Chandra Datta and Jagat Mohini Devi, of the Kayastha caste, and says the family had been Vaishnava since Chaitanya's time, although his great-grandfather turned to Shakti worship. He was a third of six children. Wikipedia says three of his brothers died of cholera and his father died soon afterward, and that a later epidemic at Ula, dated 1856 or 1857, killed his sister. He was educated at an English school in Krishnanagar. His maternal uncle took him to Calcutta in 1852, where he studied at the Hindu Charitable Institute and then Hindu College, and he learned Urdu, Persian and Sanskrit. As a young man he read widely in Western writing, including Unitarian and Transcendentalist authors, and was for a time drawn to the Brahmo Samaj. Wikipedia adds that a physician of the Kartabhaja sect treated him and gave him a Krishna mantra, and that an old woman's advice to chant Rama's name stayed with him. He married Shaymani Mitra in about 1849 or 1850 (sources differ), and she died in 1861. His second wife was Bhagavati Devi. Wikipedia lists fourteen children in all, among them Bimala Prasad, born in 1874, the future Bhaktisiddhanta.

He had taught from about 1858 and been headmaster of the school at Bhadrak. He then entered the British judicial service as a deputy magistrate. Wikipedia has him entering government service in 1866 and becoming a deputy magistrate in 1868, and some sources give earlier dates. He held the office until his retirement on 4 October 1894. Postings took him through Bengal and Orissa, with long stays at Puri from 1870, where he helped oversee the Jagannath temple and ran daily Bhagavata readings. Wikipedia says he studied the Gaudiya texts there and that his commitment to Chaitanya deepened. His official duties left him time to write, and from the late 1870s he published steadily.

His first major book, *Krishna-samhita* (1879), defended Krishna against the charge, common among educated Indians and missionaries, that the Krishna of the Puranas was morally indefensible. He is reported to have sent copies to Western scholars in 1880, among them Reinhold Rost, who replied. He wrote the *Kalyana-kalpataru* (1881), *Saranagati* and *Gitavali* (both 1893) as collections of devotional songs. In 1880 he took formal initiation from Bipin Bihari Goswami, a hereditary guru of the Baghnapara family. In January 1886 the Baghnapara goswamis gave him the title Bhaktivinoda, "one who delights in devotion." The Gaudiya Math tradition holds that his instructing guru was the ascetic Jagannath Das Babaji of Vrindavan, a claim other descendants of his family do not accept.

From 1881 he edited *Sajjana-toshani*, a Bengali monthly that ran until 1909. He published the *Chaitanya-siksamrita* (1886), translated the Bhagavad Gita into Bengali with a medieval commentary, and in 1896 issued *Jaiva Dharma*, a novel-length work that presents Vaishnava teaching through conversation and story. That year he also produced an English work on Chaitanya's life and teaching, *Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, His Life and Precepts*, copies of which, by the Bhaktivinoda Institute's account, reached learned institutions abroad. Sources differ on whether it was a stand-alone biography or an English introduction to a booklet of Sanskrit verse. The Bhaktivinoda Institute describes it as a Sanskrit poem, *Sri Gauranga Lila-Smarana Mangala Stotram*, with a Sanskrit commentary and a sixty-three-page English introduction, a copy of which reached the McGill University library and which the *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society* reviewed. Wikipedia says he wrote an autobiography the same year, published in 1916.

In the late 1880s he concluded that Chaitanya's birthplace lay in a village then called Ballaldighi, which he identified with Mayapur, near the town of Navadvip. A devotional chronology gives 1887 for the identification, and Wikipedia places it after his transfer to Krishnanagar that year, and an organisation for the sacred precinct, the Navadvipa Dhama Pracarini Sabha, followed his lecture of February 1891. He also founded Nama-hatta, a network of preaching centres whose name means "marketplace of the name." A devotional chronology dates its start to 1890. Its meetings offered kirtan, lectures and shared food to householders, and one devotional account credits his son Bhaktisiddhanta with expanding it to some five hundred centres. He moved to Mayapur after retiring in 1894, took the dress of a renunciant in 1908 and died in 1914. Wikipedia gives his age at death as seventy-five and says his remains were buried at Surabhi-kunj, his property at Mayapur.

## The teaching

Bhaktivinoda taught the theology Chaitanya (1486–1534) and his Vrindavan disciples had set down. Krishna is the supreme person, the individual soul is a tiny part of him, and the relation between them is described as *achintya bhedabheda*, "inconceivable oneness and difference." Liberation is a loving service to Krishna that continues after release, and the best method in the present age is the chanting of the holy name.

His emphasis lay in how the theology was argued and spread. He answered the objections of English-educated readers in their own terms and drew the case for personal devotion from reason and ethics as well as scripture. Wikipedia describes an approach he called *adhunika-vada*, the modern approach, which sought to reconcile reason and faith, and which defended image worship while relying on a natural intuition of the divine. He rejected readings of Krishna's love-play that treated it as sexual, which the Sahajiya groups favoured, and he criticised initiation by caste and the claim of hereditary guru families to authority. He also wrote devotional verse in Bengali, collected in *Kalyana-kalpataru*. In *Jaiva Dharma* the doctrine arrives through story, and in Nama-hatta it was meant for people with jobs and families, outside the hereditary guru households and the ascetic orders. Wikipedia says Nama-hatta ran as travelling programmes modelled on the circuit court system, offering kirtan, shared food and lectures, and counts more than five hundred centres by the early twentieth century.

## Lineage

Bhaktivinoda stands in the Gaudiya line from Chaitanya through the Goswamis. His sons carried the work forward, above all Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, who founded the Gaudiya Math, and through him A. C. Bhaktivedanta Prabhupada, who founded ISKCON.

<MiniTree id="bhaktivinoda" />

The family link to Bhaktisiddhanta is documented, as is Bhaktivinoda's initiation under Bipin Bihari Goswami. The role of Jagannath Das Babaji as his instructing guru rests on the Gaudiya Math's own tradition, and descendants of his son Lalita Prasad name Bipin Bihari Goswami instead. The Mayapur site remains disputed, and Navadvip residents rejected it. A 2023 article by Ferdinando Sardella surveys the competing evidence.

## The practice

Bhaktivinoda taught the chanting of the holy name by householders, in company. The steps follow the pattern of his own life.

<ol class="steps">
  <li><div><strong>Chant the name.</strong> Repeat the Hare Krishna mantra daily on tulasi beads, as he taught his son Bhaktisiddhanta in the early 1880s.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Gather.</strong> Meet others for kirtan, in the manner of a Nama-hatta meeting, which offered song, a lecture and shared food.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Read the Bhagavata.</strong> At Puri he held daily readings. A short regular reading takes the place of a lecture for a reader alone.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Sing his songs.</strong> The <i>Kalyana-kalpataru</i> and <i>Saranagati</i> give devotional lyrics in Bengali for use in kirtan.</div></li>
</ol>
