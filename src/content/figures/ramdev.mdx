## Life

Ramdev was born Ram Kisan Yadav in Alipur, a village in Haryana's Mahendragarh district, to farming parents, Ram Niwas and Gulabo Devi. The date most often given is 25 December 1965, but his birth year is uncertain and some accounts put it as late as the mid-1970s.

He went to a government school until about the eighth standard. Reading *Satyarth Prakash*, the founding text of Dayananda Saraswati's Arya Samaj, turned him towards the traditional schooling the Samaj promoted, and he studied Sanskrit and the Vedic texts in gurukuls in Haryana, including Arsh Gurukul Mahavidyalaya in the late 1980s. In these years he met Balkrishna, who became his lifelong partner in business and Ayurveda. In 1992 he took the vows of a sannyasi at Kripalu Bagh Ashram in Haridwar and the name Ramdev.

On 5 January 1995 Ramdev, Balkrishna and Acharya Karamveer, who had taught Ramdev yoga, founded the Divya Yog Mandir Trust in Haridwar. Ramdev began appearing on the devotional channel Sanskar TV in 2002, and in 2003 Aastha TV gave him its early-morning yoga slot. The format, a saffron-robed monk leading a stadium-sized crowd through breathing exercises, made him a household figure. In 2008 *Mail & Guardian* reported that more than twenty million people watched him daily. His residential camps drew large crowds, and in 2006 he and Balkrishna founded Patanjali Ayurved, of which Balkrishna holds most of the shares while Ramdev is the public face.

He moved into public life through campaigns against corruption and untaxed money held abroad. On 4 June 2011 he began a hunger strike at Ramlila Maidan in Delhi. Police cleared the ground after midnight with tear gas and batons, and he was flown back to Haridwar. In 2014 he backed Narendra Modi for prime minister and campaigned alongside BJP leaders. In 2015 the BJP government of Haryana named him its brand ambassador for yoga and Ayurveda with cabinet rank, which he declined.

## The teaching

Ramdev presents yoga as the system of the sage Patanjali, whose name his institutions carry, and he says it is a complete science of body, mind and spirit. "Yoga is self-healing and self-realisation," he told an interviewer in 2008. In practice his emphasis falls almost wholly on the breath. His organisation describes the balance as ninety per cent breathing and ten per cent posture.

The daily routine he teaches is a set of seven breathing exercises that can be done at home, without a teacher, in under an hour. Two are the core. *Anulom vilom* is alternate-nostril breathing, closing one nostril and then the other. *Kapalbhati*, "skull-shining," is a series of sharp, forced exhalations from the belly. Others include *bhramari*, a humming exhale with the ears closed, and *udgeeth*, a long chanted *Om*. He wrote these up in *Pranayama Rahasya* (2009).

What set the teaching apart was its promise of health. Ramdev has said for two decades that regular pranayama can treat or cure a long list of conditions, from asthma, diabetes and high blood pressure to thyroid disease, hepatitis and, in his strongest claims, cancer. In 2008 he cited testimonies collected from his followers as evidence of cancer cures; he did not cite controlled trials. He joins this to a programme of national renewal: a disciplined, vegetarian, self-reliant India using its own medicine and its own products.

## Lineage

<MiniTree id="ramdev" />

Ramdev claims no single guru line of the kind most figures in this book carry. His education links him to **Dayananda Saraswati**'s Arya Samaj, and his practice to the old yoga traditions in general. His closest associate is **Acharya Balkrishna**, managing director of Patanjali Ayurved.

## Critiques and questions

**Medical claims.** Doctors and rationalists have challenged his cure claims since the 2000s. In 2008 the Indian Medical Association's president called his cancer and HIV claims false hope for the sick, and the rationalist Sanal Edamaruku argued they broke the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954. Ramdev said in reply that his critics had an agenda. In June 2020 Patanjali launched Coronil as a treatment for Covid-19. The central government allowed it to be sold only as an immunity booster, and Patanjali withdrew the cure claim. In 2021 Ramdev called modern medicine a "stupid science" in a video; he withdrew the remark and apologised after the health minister wrote to him.

**The Supreme Court case, 2022–2024.** In 2022 the Indian Medical Association petitioned the Supreme Court over Patanjali advertisements that attacked modern medicine and claimed cures for diseases. In November 2023 Patanjali gave the Court an undertaking to stop such advertisements. In February 2024 the Court issued contempt notice to the company and to Balkrishna, finding that the advertisements had continued, and in March it ordered Ramdev and Balkrishna to appear in person. A Patanjali affidavit said the offending lines were included inadvertently and that the media team had not known of the Court's order. The bench of Justices Hima Kohli and Ahsanuddin Amanullah rejected the first apology affidavits as conditional. On 16 April 2024 Ramdev and Balkrishna apologised unconditionally in person, and Patanjali published apologies in newspapers. In April 2024 Uttarakhand's licensing authority suspended manufacturing licences for fourteen Patanjali and Divya Pharmacy products; it withdrew that order on 1 July 2024 and issued a fresh notice. On 13 August 2024 the Court accepted the apologies and closed the contempt proceedings against Patanjali, Balkrishna and Ramdev, warning that it would reopen them if its orders were breached again. No court found either man guilty of an offence in these proceedings.

**Other cases.** Kerala drug inspectors filed complaints under the 1954 Act against Divya Pharmacy, Balkrishna and Ramdev. In April 2025 the Kerala High Court stayed one such prosecution, pending before a magistrate in Palakkad, for three months.

**Public statements.** He has drawn criticism for remarks on other groups, including his 2013 statement that homosexuality is a disease that can be cured, and for remarks about Muslims in 2023 and 2025. In 2025 the Delhi High Court criticised his "sharbat jihad" comments about a rival drinks brand, and the case was closed after he undertook to remove the material.
