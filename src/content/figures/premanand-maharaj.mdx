## Life

Aniruddh Kumar Pandey was born in Akhri, a village in the Sarsaul block of Kanpur district in Uttar Pradesh. Sources disagree on the year: 1969, 1971 and 1972 all appear in print. His family were Brahmins. The ashram's biography says his grandfather was a sannyasi, his father Shambhu Pandey later took sannyasa as well, and his mother Rama Devi was devoted to visiting holy men. In the fifth class he began reading *Shri Sukhsagar*, a Hindi retelling of the Bhagavata published by Gita Press, and by the ninth he had told his mother he wanted a spiritual life.

He left home at thirteen, at three in the morning by the ashram's account. He took the vow of a *naishthik brahmachari*, a lifelong celibate, under the name Anandswaroop Brahmachari, and later took sannyasa as Swami Anandashram. For years he lived on the banks of the Ganga, at Assi Ghat in Varanasi and at other ghats between Haridwar and Varanasi, practising what he calls *akash vritti*, living only on what came to him unasked. In his own telling a vision of Shiva in Varanasi turned him toward Vrindavan.

The ashram adds that a fellow monk urged him to attend a month of devotional plays, with the life of Chaitanya in the mornings and the Ras Lila of Radha and Krishna at night. Soon after he took a train to Mathura. In Vrindavan he walked the pilgrim circuit and went daily to the Banke Bihari temple until a sadhu sent him to the Radhavallabh temple. There, the ashram says, Hit Mohit Maral Goswami gave him the sect's initiation mantra and introduced him to Hit Gaurangi Sharan Ji Maharaj, whom he took as his guru. He served Gaurangi Sharan for about ten years and lived on *madhukari*, food begged door to door from the households of Braj. The dates of these events are not documented. ThePrint, writing in 2025, placed his arrival in Vrindavan about twenty years earlier, when he was "one among thousands of other sants in the holy town."

He began holding small gatherings, and they grew. He now speaks from Shri Hit Radha Keli Kunj, an ashram at Varaha Ghat run by the Shri Hit Radha Keli Kunj Trust. The daily satsang starts before dawn. In the sessions called *Ekantik Vartalap*, a sevak reads out written questions from a small audience admitted by free token, and he answers each in a few minutes. Recordings of these sessions, posted on the ashram's Bhajan Marg channels, made him a national figure in the 2020s. ThePrint reported in August 2025 more than 30 million Instagram followers and more than 14 million YouTube subscribers, and crowds of over eight lakh on Nirjala Ekadashi that June. The cricketer Virat Kohli and the actor Anushka Sharma are among the public figures who have visited him on camera.

For some years he walked at night from his residence to the ashram, and devotees lined the road to see him. He stopped the walk in July 2024 after the stampede at a satsang in Hathras, and it has been resumed and halted again since then. He has spoken publicly about long-standing kidney disease and has declined offers of donated kidneys, saying he lives by Radha's will.

<aside class="note"><strong>Hit.</strong> The word *hit* (हित), which comes before the names of the founder and of many Radhavallabh teachers, is glossed by Premanand's ashram as pure, selfless love, the foundation of the sect's devotion.</aside>

## The teaching

The Radhavallabh Sampradaya was founded at Vrindavan by Hit Harivansh; the founding is usually dated 1535. Its poetry is in Braj Bhasha, and its main text is his *Hit Chaurasi*, eighty-four hymns to Radha. Its theology puts Radha first. Krishna is her lover and servant, and the devotee's goal is to witness their unending love play, *nitya vihar*, as one of Radha's companions. The main temple in Vrindavan has no image of Radha. A throne, the *gaddi*, placed beside Krishna stands for her presence. Premanand's own initiation from his guru, the ashram says, was into this companion's attitude, *sahachari bhav*.

What he teaches in public is simpler and aimed at householders. The remedy he gives for almost every problem is *naam jap*, repeating the names of Radha and Krishna, which ThePrint summed up as recommending "only naam jaap" as a solution. He prescribes no paid rituals and ties the name to conduct. He tells listeners to give up meat and alcohol and to keep sexual discipline before and within marriage, and he urges people to keep repeating the name while doing their ordinary work. He discourages superstition and has told followers that a holy man's blessing will not cure their bodies. Within these rules he can be practical. ThePrint reported him telling parents to support their children's choice of a partner, including across caste.

Surrender, *sharanagati*, runs through all of it. The mantra of his first Radhavallabh initiation was the mantra of taking refuge, and he describes his own life as lived by Radha's will. The devotee does the practice and leaves the result to her.

## Lineage

Premanand belongs to a defined sect and names a living chain of teachers. His initiations by Hit Mohit Maral Goswami and Hit Gaurangi Sharan rest on the ashram's own account and his talks, which no independent source contradicts.

<MiniTree id="premanand-maharaj" />

The line runs from Hit Harivansh through the Radhavallabh goswamis and renunciants of Vrindavan. One text dear to the sect, the Sanskrit *Radha Rasa Sudha Nidhi*, is attributed by Radhavallabhis to Hit Harivansh and by Gaudiya Vaishnavas to Prabodhananda Sarasvati, a dispute studied by the scholar Jagadananda Das.

## Critiques and questions

**Remarks on women, 2025.** In late July 2025 a short clip spread in which, answering a question about marriage, he said that only a few girls in a hundred are pure today, and criticised live-in relationships and multiple partners. Social media users and some religious figures called the remarks regressive. In Mathura the Shri Krishna Janmabhoomi Sangharsh Nyas demanded an apology, Amar Ujala reported on 30 July. Supporters, among them the actor Ankita Lokhande, said the clip had been cut from a longer answer, and one news report noted that he had also criticised men with many partners. No statement from him or the ashram withdrawing the remarks was found.

**Rambhadracharya, 2025.** In August 2025 Swami Rambhadracharya of Chitrakoot said in an interview that Premanand was neither a scholar nor a miracle-worker, and was reported by IANS as saying, "I challenge Premanand ji to speak even one word of Sanskrit in front of me." Several saints in Ayodhya and Ujjain objected to the remark. Rambhadracharya then said he had made no improper comment and that Premanand was like a son to him.
