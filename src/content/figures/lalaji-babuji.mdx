## Life

The tradition begins with a Hindu who was taught by a Sufi. Ram Chandra of Fatehgarh, called Lalaji, was born on 2 February 1873, on Basant Panchami, in a family of former landlords who had lost most of their property after 1857. He worked as a clerk in the collector's office at Fatehgarh, retired in 1929 and died on 14 August 1931. His teacher was Maulana Fazl Ahmad Khan of Raipur in the Farrukhabad district, a Naqshbandi-Mujaddidi shaykh. The scholar Thomas Dahnhardt, in *Change and Continuity in Indian Sufism* (2002), traces Lalaji's line this way. One online summary of Dahnhardt says Fazl Ahmad Khan named Lalaji his successor in 1896, which drew criticism from some Muslim leaders because Lalaji was not a Muslim. The Ramashram Satsang of Mathura gives the teacher's name as Maulana Shah Fazal Ahmed Khan and says Lalaji was the first Hindu to receive from him the transmission Sufis call *tawajjuh*. The Shri Ram Chandra Mission's own pages call Lalaji the rediscoverer of *pranahuti*, "the transmission of divine energy," and do not name his teacher in the pages consulted for this entry.

Ram Chandra of Shahjahanpur, called Babuji, was born on 30 April 1899 into a well-off family and left school after matriculation. He worked for thirty-one years as a record keeper in the local court. In June 1922 he met Lalaji. The Mission's biography says Lalaji had seen him in a dream and recognised him as his successor. Babuji later described his master's final message as "I became 'Thee' and thou 'I'." After Lalaji's death, Babuji gave transmission in towns across India, and in 1945 he founded and registered the Shri Ram Chandra Mission, named for his teacher. He wrote *Commentary on the Ten Maxims of Sahaj Marg* in 1946 and *Reality at Dawn* in 1954. He died on 19 April 1983.

## The teaching

Babuji called the method *Sahaj Marg*, "the natural path." It is a form of Raja Yoga, and the Mission's pages describe daily practice in four parts: relaxation, meditation focused on the heart, a practice called "cleaning" and prayer. The Mission says the cleaning removes the *samskaras*, the impressions left by past experience, and it ends the day with a prayer at night.

The distinctive claim is *pranahuti*, which means roughly "offering of the life-force." The Mission describes it as a master's transfer of spiritual energy to the practitioner's heart, and it says this feature is unique to the method. English Wikipedia attributes that claim to the Mission's sources. Babuji's Ten Maxims, which his 1946 commentary discusses, are guidance for living, among them rising early, aiming at oneness with God, simplifying one's life, accepting hardship, forgiving wrongs and examining one's conduct each night.

## Lineage

The tree shows a line from a Naqshbandi shaykh to Lalaji, to Babuji, to Parthasarathi Rajagopalachari (1927 to 2014), known as Chariji, and to Kamlesh Patel (born 1956), known as Daaji, who is the present head of the Mission and of its Heartfulness programme.

<MiniTree id="lalaji-babuji" />

The line is contested at three points, and the contests are described here as the parties describe them.

First, Lalaji's heirs. The Shri Ram Chandra Mission says Babuji was Lalaji's chosen successor. The Ramashram Satsang in Mathura, founded in 1930 at Etah by Chaturbhuj Sahay (1883 to 1957), also names itself after Lalaji, calls Lalaji its guru and describes Sahay as a senior disciple. Wikipedia lists a third body, Ram Samadhi Ashram, among organisations that trace themselves to Lalaji. Each regards its own teacher as the true bearer of his work.

Second, Babuji's heirs. The Mission says Babuji appointed Chariji, who became president in 1983 and led until his death on 20 December 2014. Court records describe a rival claim. In Supreme Court proceedings that ended on 29 April 2008, the side led by Umeshchandra Saxena argued that a 1982 nomination named Saxena as spiritual successor and president and superseded any earlier nomination, and that Chariji's nomination had been obtained by fraud. Chariji's side said the nominations were valid. The Court said it did not need to decide the meaning of the relevant sections of the Societies Registration Act and directed that the pending civil suit be decided within six months. It did not rule on the spiritual succession. The sources consulted do not report how that suit ended.

Third, Chariji's heir. Chariji, according to the Mission, named Kamlesh Patel as successor in October 2011. Patel became vice president in August 2012 and guide in December 2014. The Mission now uses the name Heartfulness for its programmes.
