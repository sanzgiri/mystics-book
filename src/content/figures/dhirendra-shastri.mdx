## Life

Dhirendra Krishna Garg was born on 4 July 1996 in Gada, a village in Chhatarpur district in the Bundelkhand region of Madhya Pradesh. His father, Ram Kripal Garg, was a priest, and his mother is Saroj Garg. Accounts describe a poor Brahmin household and a boy who recited kathas to villagers while still at school in nearby Ganj. The family served a small Hanuman shrine at Gada, Bageshwar Dham, which they say was founded by his ancestor Sannyasi Baba, described in some accounts as his great-grandfather. Reports describe Dhirendra as the third generation of the family to lead it, after his grandfather and father.

He became known as Dhirendra Krishna Shastri, and his devotees call him Bageshwar Dham Sarkar or Bageshwar Baba. He describes himself as a disciple of Jagadguru Rambhadracharya, the scholar-monk of Chitrakoot. From the late 2010s videos of his twice-weekly darbars circulated widely on YouTube and devotional television, and by 2023 he was drawing very large crowds to katha programmes across north India and abroad. Wikipedia counted about 7.5 million followers across his social media accounts.

The shrine has grown with him. It runs a free kitchen, holds an annual mass wedding for brides from poor families and is building a Vedic school. On 23 February 2025 Prime Minister Narendra Modi laid the foundation stone of the Bageshwar Dham Medical and Science Research Institute, a cancer hospital planned on ten acres at Gada with a first phase of 100 beds and free treatment for poor patients. Modi called Shastri "my younger brother Dhirendra Shastri."

## The teaching

Shastri's teaching is devotion to Hanuman, the servant of Rama, whom he calls Bageshwar Balaji, and to Rama through the *Ramcharitmanas* of Tulsidas. He presents himself as a servant of the deity. "I am just a servant at the feet of Bageshwar Balaji," he said in 2023 when his powers were questioned. His kathas mix scripture, songs and moral instruction on family life, vegetarianism and loyalty to Sanatan Dharma, his preferred name for Hinduism.

The divya darbar, or divine court, held every Tuesday and Saturday, is what made him famous. Visitors register for a token, and a few are called to the stage. Shastri writes on a slip of paper, the *parcha*, what he says is the visitor's problem, often as soon as he looks at them, and sometimes announces names and family details aloud. Devotees take this as proof that he knows their troubles without being told. He also offers remedies for illness, family trouble and what devotees describe as possession by spirits. He says the knowledge comes from Hanuman, from his own sadhana or spiritual discipline, and from the blessing of Sannyasi Baba. "I have faith in my God," he told reporters in January 2023.

He joins devotion to a political programme. He has called repeatedly for India to be declared a Hindu Rashtra, a Hindu nation, and for the *Ramcharitmanas* to be made a national scripture. On 23 January 2023, as widely reported, he told a gathering in a Hindi slogan that if they supported him he would give them a Hindu Rashtra. He has run ceremonies to bring converts to Christianity back to Hinduism, which supporters call *ghar wapsi*, homecoming; the RSS weekly *Organiser* reported that 220 people from Christian families of the Bundelkhand area were brought back at one such ceremony at Bageshwar Dham in February 2023. He has also preached against caste division among Hindus. In November 2024 he led a 160-kilometre, nine-day Sanatan Hindu Ekta Padyatra, a unity march, from Bageshwar Dham to Orchha. "The purpose of this yatra is to unite Hindus by removing the caste system and the animosity that divides us," he said at its start. A second march, from Delhi to Vrindavan, followed in November 2025.

## Lineage

<MiniTree id="dhirendra-shastri" />

Shastri's authority comes from two lines. One is family: the shrine and its powers, as he tells it, descend from **Sannyasi Baba** through his grandfather and father, a devotional claim. The other is his discipleship to **Jagadguru Rambhadracharya**, a documented relationship that ties him to the Ramanandi Vaishnava world of Rama devotion.

## Critiques and questions

**The Nagpur challenge, 2023.** In January 2023, while Shastri was holding a katha in Nagpur scheduled for 5 to 13 January, Shyam Manav of the Akhil Bharatiya Andhashraddha Nirmoolan Samiti, a rationalist group that campaigns against superstition, offered him Rs 30 lakh if he could correctly name ten people chosen by the group, with their phone numbers and other details. Shastri left Nagpur two days before the scheduled end, for Raipur; his side said this was his schedule, and Manav said he had fled. Manav filed a police complaint under Maharashtra's 2013 anti-superstition and black magic law and the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act. On 25 January 2023 the Nagpur police commissioner, Amitesh Kumar, said "the programme held by Maharaj in Nagpur does not attract action under any these laws," and no case was registered. Manav said the police had yielded to political pressure. Shastri answered from Raipur. "Such people will keep coming. We do not operate within closed doors," he said, inviting critics to come and watch on camera. Manav says his group is "against fraud or exploitation in the name of god." In April 2026, during a second katha in Nagpur, Shastri again invited doubters, and Manav raised his offer to Rs 80 lakh. The reports available do not record that the test was ever held.

**How the readings work.** According to *DailyO*, visitors who want to meet him first take a token through the shrine's website, giving their own, their father's and their children's names and a mobile number. At a darbar in Raipur on 20 January 2023 Shastri named relatives of an ABP News reporter, Gyanendra Tiwari, and the channel aired it as a live miracle. The fact-checking site BOOM found that the relatives' names and the family housewarming he mentioned were on the reporter's public Facebook profile, and an ABP News executive told BOOM, "The information is available on Facebook, there is nothing astonishing about this." Shastri says his knowledge comes from Balaji and invites anyone to test him on camera.

**Statements on religion and community.** His remarks have repeatedly drawn criticism and complaints. In March 2023 Udaipur police registered an FIR over his call to replace green flags with saffron at Kumbhalgarh Fort, on the allegation that it could promote enmity between communities; the reports available do not record its outcome. In April 2023 he said Shirdi Sai Baba could be a saint or a fakir but not God, which drew protests from Sai devotees. In a clarification on 5 April 2023 he said he had always respected saints, that he deeply regretted it if any word of his had hurt anyone, and that if people regarded a saint or guru as God out of personal faith, Bageshwar Dham had no objection. In November 2023, after remarks about the Marathi saint Tukaram angered members of the Warkari community and his volunteers clashed with Tukaram's followers at his discourse in Pune, he said he had the deepest respect for Tukaram and regretted his past comments if they had caused hurt. Political opponents, among them Asaduddin Owaisi, have said his Hindu Rashtra demand is contrary to the Constitution; Uttar Pradesh chief minister Yogi Adityanath, according to Wikipedia's account, endorsed his remarks and said India was already a Hindu Rashtra. In September 2026 his advice to Bengali Hindus to avoid meat during Durga Puja was criticised across parties in West Bengal, including by the state's BJP president, Samik Bhattacharya, who said food was a personal choice.

**His position.** He has said he respects saints, that he does not mean to hurt anyone's faith, and that he does not intend to enter electoral politics. Published reports record no criminal charge against him over the darbars.
