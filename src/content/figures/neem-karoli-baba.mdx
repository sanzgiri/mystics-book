## Life

Very little about his early life rests on documents. He is said to have been born as Lakshman Narayan Sharma (some sources write Lakshmi) around 1900 in Akbarpur, in Uttar Pradesh. Sources disagree on which district the village lies in, naming Firozabad in some places and Ambedkar Nagar in others. Devotees say his parents married him at eleven, that he left home soon after to wander as a sadhu devoted to Hanuman, and that he returned at his father's request and had two sons and a daughter. In those years he went by several names, among them Lakshman Das, Handi Wallah Baba and Tikonia Wallah Baba.

The best-known story gives the name by which he is known. Devotees tell of a young sadhu who boarded a first-class train without a ticket and was put off at a village called Neeb Karori. The train would not move until officials asked him to board again, and he agreed on condition that the village get a station. The ashram that tells the story presents it as a traditional account. The name Neem Karoli Baba is said to come from it. His followers called him Maharaj-ji, "great king."

From the early 1960s his base was in the Kumaon hills. According to the history of the Kainchi ashram, he and a local man, Poornanand, chose the forest site near Nainital in 1962, and a statue of Hanuman was consecrated there on 15 June 1964, which is still marked every year. He also kept a place at Vrindavan, and devotees count more than one hundred Hanuman temples built in his name.

In 1967 Richard Alpert, an American psychologist who had lost his Harvard post over LSD research, was brought to Kainchi by a young American seeker, Bhagavan Das. In Ram Dass's later telling, Maharaj-ji told him that he had been thinking of his mother under the stars the night before and that she had died the previous year, and then said in English, "Spleen!" Alpert's mother had died of leukemia after an operation to remove her spleen. The next day Maharaj-ji asked for the "medicine," and by Ram Dass's account took LSD and showed no apparent effect. He is said to have told Alpert that the same state can be reached through meditation. He gave Alpert the name Ram Dass, "servant of Ram."

Ram Dass published *Be Here Now* in 1971 through the Lama Foundation in New Mexico. Other Westerners also went to India, among them the musician Krishna Das and the physician Larry Brilliant. Maharaj-ji died early on 11 September 1973 at a hospital in Vrindavan, after a diabetic coma, while returning from Agra, where he had gone to see a heart specialist. Devotees report that his last words were "Jaya Jagadish Hare." His ashrams at Kainchi and Vrindavan remain pilgrimage sites. The Uttarakhand government has reported about 2.4 million visitors to Kainchi in 2024.

## The teaching

He wrote no books and gave no discourses that were recorded in his lifetime. His devotees remember short instructions and actions. The one most often repeated is "Love everyone, serve everyone, remember God, and tell the truth." The Taos ashram that preserves his teaching gives his core idea as *sab ek*, "all is one." *Miracle of Love*, Ram Dass's collection of stories about him, carries the line "There can be no biography of him. Facts are few, stories many." in its publisher's description.

His practice was Hanuman devotion, the ideal of a servant who has given himself fully to Ram. Devotees stress feeding people as worship. Seva, service to others, was the way the devotion showed itself. One account says he taught in riddles and parables and not in systematic form.

<aside class="note"><strong>Hanuman.</strong> In the Ramayana, Hanuman is Ram's monkey servant, strong, humble and devoted. In north India he is a main figure of popular worship, and the ideal he stands for is service without ego.</aside>

Miracle stories fill the devotional literature. Followers say he could be in two places at once, knew people's thoughts and put devotees into samadhi with a touch. These are reported by devotees, mainly in *Miracle of Love*, and are not independently verified.

## Lineage

No guru is named for him in the sources consulted, and the devotional literature centres on his devotion to Hanuman. He is placed in the Ram and Hanuman bhakti current. His best-known disciple was Ram Dass (1931–2019), who co-founded the Hanuman Foundation in 1974 and, with Larry Brilliant, the Seva Foundation in 1978.
