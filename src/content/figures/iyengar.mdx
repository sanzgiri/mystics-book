## Life

Iyengar was born on 14 December 1918, the eleventh of thirteen children of a schoolteacher by Wikipedia's account (*Encyclopedia.com* calls him the thirteenth child). His mother had influenza while carrying him, in the pandemic year. He was a weak child with tuberculosis, malaria, malnutrition and typhoid or typhus (Wikipedia says the one, the Iyengar institute the other). His father died of appendicitis just before his ninth birthday. Doctors had predicted he would not live to twenty-one, according to the reference work *Encyclopedia.com*. After his father's death he moved to Bangalore to live with a brother, and he failed his English examination, which closed the way to college.

His sister had married T. Krishnamacharya, the yoga teacher of the Mysore palace. The institute's account says the boy went to live with them at fourteen. Wikipedia dates the move to 1934. Krishnamacharya at first doubted that the boy could meet the physical demands, and gave him household chores. According to *Encyclopedia.com*, Iyengar won him over by learning a set of postures quickly and performing them perfectly when a student failed to appear before a demonstration. Krishnamacharya set him postures to improve his health, and Wikipedia adds that he sometimes withheld food until a student had mastered a pose. By Iyengar's account the teaching he got was slight and the treatment harsh, and Krishnamacharya predicted he would fail. Iyengar called the Mysore years "the major turning point in my life."

By then he had given his first class and travelled with Krishnamacharya on demonstration tours across Karnataka. A group of women asked for a teacher of their own, and Krishnamacharya sent Iyengar, the youngest of his students, who went from village to village on a diet of rice and water. In 1937, at eighteen, he was sent to Pune to teach. Rashmi Palkhivala, his biographer, writes that he lived in a hotel, that a doctor named Gokhale arranged for colleges and the Deccan Gymkhana to send pupils, and that there were only ten students in all. After a forty-minute demonstration of advanced postures at the Gymkhana in 1937, she says, the audience was won over. He married Ramamani in 1943. She was one of his first students, and the institute says he developed his way of teaching by teaching her.

His reach grew from the 1950s. He showed postures to India's president Rajendra Prasad in 1950, met the violinist Yehudi Menuhin in 1952 and was invited to Switzerland in 1954. The meeting with Menuhin was planned for five minutes and ran over three hours, and led to classes. Menuhin introduced him to musicians in Switzerland and England, and the Belgian Queen Mother became a student. He first taught in the United States in 1956. *Light on Yoga* appeared in 1966 with a foreword by Menuhin. Ramamani died in January 1973, and the Ramamani Iyengar Memorial Yoga Institute opened in Pune in 1975. His daughter Geeta, born in 1944, and his son Prashant became well-known teachers and now co-direct the Pune institute. He retired from full-time teaching in 1984, and at ninety he reported practising asanas for three hours and pranayama for one hour each day. *Time* named him among the hundred most influential people of 2004. He received the Padma Shri in 1991, the Padma Bhushan in 2002 and the Padma Vibhushan in 2014. He died in Pune on 20 August 2014. Newspapers dated the report 20 and 21 August.

## The teaching

His one "guru mantra," the institute's story says, was "Perfection in asana." Accounts of the method stress precise alignment, a deliberate sequence of postures and props such as belts, blocks and ropes, and teachers often add timing, which means holding each posture for some length. Wikipedia cites Mehta, Mehta and Mehta (1990) for the claim that props were pioneered in Iyengar Yoga within modern postural yoga. Props let beginners, the old and the injured do postures that would otherwise be out of reach. The *Encyclopedia.com* entry lists weights, pulleys, stools and ropes among them, and Wikipedia says Iyengar began using props to help people with disabilities after a scooter accident dislocated his spine. The institute that certifies his teachers describes the method as one whose "focus on precision, timing, sequencing, and the use of props helps the practitioner develop awareness in any asana." Only certified Iyengar Yoga teachers may describe their instruction as Iyengar Yoga, and progress is meant to be gradual and steady.

He gave breathing its own book, *Light on Pranayama*, which appeared in 1981, and the same sources list *The Tree of Yoga* (first published 1988) and *Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health* (2001). Wikipedia reports that *Light on Yoga* became a bestseller in 17 languages, with three million copies sold by 2005. It describes some 200 postures with about 600 photographs of Iyengar, and sets hatha yoga as one of the eight limbs of Patanjali's yoga. It opens with a prayer to Patanjali. In *Light on Life*, as quoted by NPR in 2005, he called practice for health and flexibility "the external practice of yoga" and wrote, "While this is a legitimate place to begin, it is not the end." He described three quests: the external, which builds firmness of the body, the internal, which steadies intelligence, and the innermost, which brings benevolence of spirit. Suzanne Newcombe, as Wikipedia summarises her, notes that he asked students to adopt no particular beliefs. His claim is that careful work on the body is itself a route inward.

### Questions raised

Andrea Jain and others note that *Light on Yoga* states medical benefits without supporting evidence. A reviewer of the book found it silent on contraindications. Wikipedia also records a claim by K. Pattabhi Jois that he had taught Iyengar, which Iyengar denied.

## Lineage

Iyengar is the third of the three great students of Krishnamacharya shown in this part.

<MiniTree id="iyengar" />

The tie to Krishnamacharya is documented: Iyengar was his brother-in-law and lived in his household in Mysore. Iyengar's own pupils include his children Geeta and Prashant, and certified Iyengar teachers now work in many countries. Krishnamacharya's other great student, Pattabhi Jois, kept the flowing sequences of the Mysore palace. Iyengar took the same postures into a slower form with long holds and props.

## The practice

<ol class="steps">
  <li><div><strong>Find a certified teacher.</strong> The Iyengar associations say that only certified teachers may call their instruction Iyengar Yoga, and that certification follows training and assessment.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Begin with a few postures.</strong> The method is described as open to all ages and body types, with progress gradual and steady.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Work on one alignment at a time.</strong> Each posture is held with attention to placement of the body, which the institute names as precision.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Use a prop when the posture is out of reach.</strong> Belts, blocks and ropes let beginners, older people and the injured do postures that would otherwise be out of reach.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Extend the hold with time.</strong> Timing and sequencing are named parts of the method, so how long a posture is held and in what order the postures come are decisions the teacher makes.</div></li>
  <li><div><strong>Study the breath separately.</strong> Iyengar wrote a separate book on pranayama (1981), and at ninety said he practised it for an hour a day.</div></li>
</ol>
