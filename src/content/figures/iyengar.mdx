## Life

Iyengar was born on 14 December 1918, the eleventh of thirteen children of a schoolteacher. His mother had influenza while carrying him, in the pandemic year. He was a weak child with tuberculosis, malaria, malnutrition and typhoid or typhus (Wikipedia says the one, the Iyengar institute the other). His father died of appendicitis just before his ninth birthday.

His sister had married T. Krishnamacharya, the yoga teacher of the Mysore palace. The institute's account says the boy went to live with them at fourteen. Wikipedia dates the move to 1934. Krishnamacharya set him postures to improve his health. By Iyengar's account the teaching he got was slight and the treatment harsh, and by Iyengar's account Krishnamacharya predicted he would fail. Iyengar called the Mysore years "the major turning point in my life."

In 1937, at eighteen, he was sent to Pune to teach. Rashmi Palkhivala, his biographer, writes that he lived in a hotel, that a doctor named Gokhale arranged for colleges and the Deccan Gymkhana to send pupils, and that there were only ten students in all. After a forty-minute demonstration of advanced postures at the Gymkhana in 1937, she says, the audience was won over. He married Ramamani in 1943. She was one of his first students, and the institute says he developed his way of teaching by teaching her.

His reach grew from the 1950s. He showed postures to India's president Rajendra Prasad in 1950, met the violinist Yehudi Menuhin in 1952 and was invited to Switzerland in 1954. He first taught in the United States in 1956. *Light on Yoga* appeared in 1966 with a foreword by Menuhin. Ramamani died in January 1973, and the Ramamani Iyengar Memorial Yoga Institute opened in Pune in 1975. His daughter Geeta and son Prashant became well-known teachers. *Time* named him among the hundred most influential people of 2004. He received the Padma Shri in 1991, the Padma Bhushan in 2002 and the Padma Vibhushan in 2014. He died in Pune on 20 August 2014. Newspapers dated the report 20 and 21 August.

## The teaching

His one "guru mantra," the institute's story says, was "Perfection in asana." Accounts of the method stress precise alignment, a deliberate sequence of postures and props such as belts, blocks and ropes, and teachers often add timing, which means holding each posture for some length. Wikipedia cites Mehta, Mehta and Mehta (1990) for the claim that props were pioneered in Iyengar Yoga within modern postural yoga. Props let beginners, the old and the injured do postures that would otherwise be out of reach.

*Light on Yoga* describes some 200 postures with about 600 photographs of Iyengar, and sets hatha yoga as one of the eight limbs of Patanjali's yoga. It opens with a prayer to Patanjali. In *Light on Life*, as quoted by NPR in 2005, he called practice for health and flexibility "the external practice of yoga" and wrote, "While this is a legitimate place to begin, it is not the end." He described three quests: the external, which builds firmness of the body, the internal, which steadies intelligence, and the innermost, which brings benevolence of spirit. Suzanne Newcombe, as Wikipedia summarises her, notes that he asked students to adopt no particular beliefs. His claim is that careful work on the body is itself a route inward.

### Questions raised

Writers have described a severe teaching style. The journalist Michelle Goldberg writes that he barked orders and yelled at students, who nicknamed him "Bang, Kick, Slap" from his initials, and that he justified this as the price of high standards. Andrea Jain and others note that *Light on Yoga* states medical benefits without supporting evidence. A reviewer of the book found it silent on contraindications. Wikipedia also records a claim by K. Pattabhi Jois that he, and not Krishnamacharya, was Iyengar's guru.
