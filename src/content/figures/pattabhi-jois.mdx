## Life

Krishna Pattabhi Jois was born on 26 July 1915 at Kowshika, a village near Hassan in what is now Karnataka. In 1927, at about twelve, he saw a lecture and demonstration by Tirumalai Krishnamacharya at the Jubilee Hall in Hassan and became his student the next day. He studied with him in Hassan for about two years. He later went to Mysore, studied Sanskrit there, and rejoined Krishnamacharya, whose yoga school in the Jaganmohan Palace was supported by the Maharaja.

From 1937 to 1973 Jois taught yoga at the Sanskrit College in Mysore, where he became a *vidwan*, a professor, in 1956. From 1976 to 1978 he was honorary professor of yoga at the Government College of Indian Medicine. In 1948 he set up the Ashtanga Yoga Research Institute in his home in the Lakshmipuram neighbourhood. His book *Yoga Mala* appeared in Kannada in 1958 and in English in 1999.

Foreign students began to arrive in the 1960s. The Belgian André Van Lysebeth spent two months with him in 1964. In the 1970s a small group of Americans followed, and in 1975 Jois spent four months teaching in Encinitas, California, the start of Ashtanga's spread in the United States. In later decades he taught large workshops in America and Europe, and thousands came to Mysore each year. The institute later took the name K. Pattabhi Jois Ashtanga Yoga Institute. His daughter Saraswathi Rangaswamy and her son R. Sharath Jois carried on the teaching. Pattabhi Jois died in Mysore on 18 May 2009, aged 93.

## The teaching

Jois called his method Ashtanga yoga, the "eight-limbed" yoga of Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras*, and said that posture practice was the door to the other limbs. The practice is a set of fixed series: a primary series, an intermediate series and advanced series. Each posture is joined to the next by *vinyasa*, a counted sequence of movements matched to inhalation and exhalation, so that the practice flows without pause and builds heat.

The method rests on *tristhana*, "three places" of attention: the breath, the posture and the *drishti*, a fixed point of gaze. Students breathe with an audible sound and hold the bandhas, the locks at the pelvic floor and the lower belly. In the Mysore style each student practises the series at their own pace in a shared room, and the teacher gives each person new postures only when the earlier ones are steady. Teacher-led classes were added in his later years. Hands-on adjustment by the teacher was a central and expected part of the class.

**The Yoga Korunta.** Jois taught that the system came from Krishnamacharya, who said he had learned it from an ancient text called the *Yoga Korunta*, attributed to a sage named Vamana and taught to him by his Himalayan guru. Krishnamacharya said the manuscript had been eaten by ants. Jois said he never saw it. The text is not cited in Krishnamacharya's own books of 1934 and about 1941, and no copy has been examined by scholars. Historians such as Mark Singleton connect the sequences with the gymnastics and physical culture taught at the Mysore palace in the 1930s. Manju Jois, Pattabhi Jois's son, has said that Krishnamacharya created the sequences.

## Critiques and questions

**Allegations about adjustments.** In April 2018 *The Walrus* published an article by Matthew Remski in which nine women described Jois touching their breasts and genitals, and pressing or rubbing his body against them, during adjustments in public classes, workshops and his Mysore shala. The incidents described run from the late 1970s or early 1980s to 2002. Several women said the adjustments also injured them, and many said that his standing and the devotional culture around him kept them from speaking. One of them, Karen Rain, later published her own account. The teacher Judith Hanson Lasater has described a similar adjustment, and other senior teachers, including Gregor Maehle and Kino MacGregor, have made statements supporting the accounts. Photographs and video of adjustments have circulated. Jois died in 2009 and was never charged with any offence. The allegations have not been tested in any court.

**Responses.** When *The Walrus* published, it reported that Sharath Jois had not responded to its requests for comment, and noted a new code of conduct for authorised teachers that required an environment free from sexual harassment. On 10 July 2019 Sharath Jois posted a statement on Instagram. He wrote that <q>I also witnessed him giving improper adjustments</q> and that <q>I am sorry that it caused pain for any of his students</q>, and he asked those harmed to forgive his grandfather. Remski later described it as a rambling personal statement and noted that it had been deleted. No public statement on the allegations by Saraswathi Rangaswamy or by the institute as such has been located for this entry. In 2019 the family's teaching divided between the K. Pattabhi Jois Ashtanga Yoga Shala, run by Saraswathi, and Sharath's new Sharath Yoga Centre. Sharath Jois died on 11 November 2024.

## Lineage

Jois is the second great branch from Krishnamacharya, beside B.K.S. Iyengar. Where Iyengar slowed the postures down and added props, Jois kept the flowing sequences of the palace years.

The edge from Krishnamacharya is documented from 1927. The claimed descent from the *Yoga Korunta* rests on Krishnamacharya's account alone. The family line continues through Saraswathi Rangaswamy and Sharath Jois.
