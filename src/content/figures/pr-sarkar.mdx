## Life

Prabhat Ranjan Sarkar was born on 21 May 1921 at Jamalpur, a railway town on the Ganges in Bihar, into a Bengali family. His father, Lakshmi Narayan Sarkar, was a homoeopathic doctor, and his mother was Abharani Devi. One profile gives 1922 for the year of birth; the movement's sources give 1921.

In 1939 he went to Calcutta to study at Vidyasagar College and left when his father's death put the family under strain. The movement's founding story belongs to that year. Sarkar described it in a later essay, *Shravani Purnima*: walking at night near Kashi Mitra Ghat on the river, he was confronted by a feared bandit, Kalicharan Bandyopadhyay, and won him over. Kalicharan became Kalikananda, counted as his first disciple. The account is Sarkar's own, published in 1980.

He went back to Jamalpur and took a job as an accountant at the railway workshops, the employer his father had worked for (the start date is 1941 in one source and 1944 in another). He gathered disciples while he kept the job. In January 1955 at Jamalpur he founded the Ananda Marga Pracaraka Samgha, the "society for propagating the path of bliss," and took the name Shrii Shrii Anandamurti, "one in the form of bliss." Its motto, *Atmamokshartham jagaddhitaya ca*, means "for liberation of self and service to all." His aphoristic summary of the path, *Ananda Sutram*, appeared in 1961. An order of monks and nuns followed in 1962, the movement built a centre at Ananda Nagar in the Purulia district of West Bengal, and relief, education and women's wings over the next few years. The first teachers went abroad in 1967.

In December 1971 he was arrested on charges of conspiracy to commit, and abetment of, the murder of six followers who had allegedly left the movement, according to a government statement to the Lok Sabha in December 1977. (One later account dates the charge to June 1971, and a 1978 American press report gave the number of victims as five.) The movement says that on 12 February 1973 he was poisoned in Bankipur Central Jail in Patna. He demanded a judicial inquiry, and from 1 April 1973 he took only a liquid diet until his release, a fast of more than five years. In July 1975, after Indira Gandhi declared the Emergency, the government banned Ananda Marga. On 29 November 1976 the Additional Sessions Judge at Patna convicted him under sections of the Indian Penal Code covering conspiracy to murder, abetment and the concealment of evidence, and sentenced him to life imprisonment, according to the Home Minister's written answer to the Lok Sabha on 13 July 1977. He appealed to the Patna High Court on 25 January 1977. On 4 July 1978 the High Court acquitted him and four followers. As reported by the UNI news agency, the court held that the prosecution had not proved the charges beyond reasonable doubt and that the approver, the former member whose testimony carried the case, was "a totally unreliable witness." He left prison on 2 August 1978. A 2017 *Outlook* article says he "was not convicted"; the parliamentary record of the sessions verdict contradicts it.

From September 1982 until his death he composed 5,018 songs in Bengali and other languages, the *Prabhat Samgiita*, "songs of the new dawn." He introduced his idea of microvita, tiny entities he described as lying between matter and mind, in lectures from 1986. He died in Calcutta on 21 October 1990. He named no successor as guru, and the organisation elected a monk, Acharya Shraddhananda Avadhuta, as its head.

## The teaching

Sarkar called tantra a spiritual science, open to anyone, with no tie to a particular religion. Its aim is to expand the mind from its bondage until it merges with the supreme consciousness, which he called Brahma or Paramapurusha. He described his philosophy as a non-dualism in which all things begin and end as one consciousness, and he rejected ritual worship and prayer in favour of personal practice.

That practice is given in person and free of charge by an *acharya*, a teacher authorised by the movement, and is done at least twice a day. It rests on the ethical code of *yama* and *niyama*, the restraints and observances of classical yoga, which he redefined in his own terms. Members keep a list of Sixteen Points covering diet, hygiene and conduct, meet weekly for collective meditation called *dharmacakra*, and chant *Baba Nam Kevalam*, "only His name." The goal is joined to the motto: personal liberation and the service of society are one path.

From that base he built a social theory. PROUT, the Progressive Utilization Theory, first set out in 1959, treats the world's resources as the common inheritance of all beings. It proposes a three-tier economy of public utilities, cooperatives and small private business, decentralised into self-reliant regional units, and holds that no one should accumulate wealth without society's permission. Neohumanism, set out in a 1982 book, extends humanism to animals, plants and the inanimate world.

## Questions raised

**The murder case.** Sarkar was charged with conspiracy in the deaths of six followers who had allegedly defected, convicted by a Patna sessions court in 1976 and acquitted on appeal by the Patna High Court in 1978. The acquittal is final. The movement calls the charges fabricated and the acquittal a "Victory of Dharma," and it holds that the alleged poisoning of 1973 was an attempt by officials on his life. No independent inquiry into the poisoning claim was found in the sources consulted.

**Violence around the movement.** On 30 April 1982 mobs in Calcutta attacked Ananda Marga monks and nuns at three places in the south of the city, Bondel Gate, the Bijon Setu bridge and Ballygunge station, and killed seventeen of them, sixteen monks and a nun. Ananda Marga blamed workers of the ruling Communist Party of India (Marxist); the chief minister, Jyoti Basu, suspected that the attack had been staged to embarrass his government before an election, according to *The Statesman*. Basu's government appointed a commission of inquiry on 4 May 1982, and in March 2012 the West Bengal government appointed a retired Calcutta High Court judge to a new commission; no findings had been published at the time of writing, and no one has been convicted. In a separate case, a district court in New Delhi on 8 December 2014 convicted four men described as Ananda Marga followers of the 1975 grenade murder of the railway minister L.N. Mishra, and on 18 December sentenced them to life imprisonment. The four maintained their innocence, and their appeals, filed in the Delhi High Court in 2015, were still pending in 2026. That case did not concern Sarkar personally.

## Lineage

Sarkar named no guru of his own. He sits on the tree as a branch of Shakta tantra, with Kalikananda as his first disciple by his own account.

<MiniTree id="pr-sarkar" />

The tantric line is plain in the practice he taught and is documented. The Kalikananda edge rests on Sarkar's own testimony. His organisational successor was elected, and the movement treats Anandamurti as its only guru.
