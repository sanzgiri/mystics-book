## Life

Ambalal Muljibhai Patel was born on 7 November 1908 in Tarsali, a village near Baroda in present-day Gujarat, according to Wikipedia. The scholar Peter Flügel, in a SOAS lecture abstract, describes him as a contractor. He lived an ordinary householder's life and kept his business.

By his own account, which the movement's booklets repeat, he was sitting on a bench on platform 3 of Surat railway station at about six o'clock on a June evening in 1958 when self-realisation came to him over the next forty-eight minutes. Wikipedia gives the same details, citing Flügel and a 2011 biography by Suresh Jani. Flügel's abstract says only that he had the experience at Surat while waiting for his train back to Bombay. This, he said, was the source of his knowledge.

He began to give what his followers call *gnan* (knowledge) to others. Wikipedia reports that Kanubhai Patel, his business partner, received it in 1963, and that the first public Gnan Vidhi took place in Bombay in 1968. According to the Wikipedia article, citing Flügel, the ceremony reached its present form in 1983, and about 50,000 followers were reported that year. His discourses were recorded between 1974 and 1988 and later edited into the *Aptavani* volumes. He died on 2 January 1988, and the same source reports about 60,000 people at his funeral.

## The teaching

Akram Vignan means "stepless science." The movement contrasts it with *kram*, the step-by-step path of renunciation, discipline and purification that the Jain tradition prescribes. Wikipedia's article on the movement says it promises liberation through a short ceremony, with the grace of Simandhar Swami. In Jain belief Simandhar Swami is a living tirthankara, a liberated teacher, in a distant realm called Mahavideha. Wikipedia describes the movement as offering salvation through a medium (*nimitta*), with A. M. Patel as the principal one. In the movement's own booklets the name Dada Bhagwan refers to the Lord within, which he said was fully manifest in him and present in all living beings.

The Gnan Vidhi is described by Wikipedia as a ritual transfer of knowledge. Followers are then said to live as householders and to observe the self as separate from the body and mind. Followers' accounts and the movement's publications are the main sources for what that practice involves, and none has been checked directly for this entry.

Flügel, as summarised on Wikipedia, regards the movement as a Jain-Vaishnava syncretism. The SOAS abstract calls it "a highly innovative religious movement" offering a new combination of Hindu and Jain ideas. Wikipedia also notes that traditional Jainism holds liberation to be impossible in the present age, which sets it apart from the movement's claim.

## After 1988

After Dada Bhagwan died, the movement divided into two groups, according to Wikipedia citing Flügel. One was led by Kanubhai Patel and backed by Jay Sachchidanand Sangh. Wikipedia says Kanubhai claimed succession on the basis of a recorded tape. The other was led by Niruben Amin (born 1944, died 2006), a doctor who said Dada Bhagwan had trained her in the Gnan Vidhi and who had travelled with him in his last years.

Niruben Amin formed the Dada Bhagwan Foundation Trust, the Simandhar Swami Aradhana Trust in Ahmedabad and the Mahavideh Foundation in Mumbai. Her followers called her Niruma from 1999. After her death in 2006, Deepakbhai Desai succeeded her. The Dada Bhagwan Foundation is therefore the organisation descended from her side of the split. Wikipedia's article on the movement lists more than twenty temples (*Trimandir*) built by it, citing Wiley (2009).

Wikipedia reports that Kanubhai Patel died in the United States in June 2020. The movement's own accounts of the split have not been consulted for this entry.

## Lineage

Dada Bhagwan had no recorded human guru, and the tree links him to the Jain tradition only as an influence. His line of succession divides after 1988 into the two groups described above.
