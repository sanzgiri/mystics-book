## Life

Vethathiri Maharishi was born on 14 August 1911 in Guduvancheri, then in the Madras Presidency, the eighth child of Varadappan and Chinnammal. Wikipedia says the family were weavers. He began school in 1916 and left because of the family's poverty. The information folder India Post issued with his stamp says he worked as an apprentice weaver and later owned a textile business with about a thousand looms. He also qualified as a practitioner of Siddha medicine, Ayurveda and homeopathy.

Wikipedia, whose sentences here are only partly sourced, says that at twelve he met a man named A. Balakrishna who introduced him to spirituality, and that the seer Paranjyothi later initiated him into kundalini yoga. The initiation is documented to a video on a movement channel, and no year is given. He was inspired by the nineteenth-century Tamil saint Ramalinga Swamigal, called Vallalar.

In 1958 he founded the World Community Service Centre in Chennai. The organisation says it is non-profit and non-religious. In 1984 he established the Arivu Thirukkoil, the Temple of Consciousness, at Aliyar in Coimbatore district. The India Post folder says he was invited to the United Nations on 9 January 1975 to speak on the value of yoga and meditation. No UN record of the talk has been located. Wikipedia says the Centre has consultative status with the UN's Economic and Social Council, a claim the organisation makes. He wrote more than 75 books (Wikipedia) or 70 (the India Post folder) and, the folder says, about 2,000 Tamil poems, and edited the monthly *Arivoli*. India Post issued a stamp for his centenary on 14 August 2010.

He died on 28 March 2006 in Coimbatore. The India Post folder gives his age as 95, but 94 follows from the dates.

## The teaching

Vethathiri held that suffering comes from not understanding oneself and one's relation to others, and that peace begins in the individual. His motto, as the movement states it, is world peace through individual peace.

The practice is Simplified Kundalini Yoga. As described in a 2022 article by two authors working in the movement's research and teaching programmes, it combines simplified physical exercises, which purify the body, meditation, introspection, which includes analysing thoughts and neutralising anger, and kayakalpa. Kayakalpa is an old south Indian rejuvenation practice, which Vethathiri's followers say he revived. The same article lists ten stages, running from the awakening of kundalini at the base of the spine to the crown centre, through the mastery of six faults (greed, anger, miserliness, uncontrolled sexual desire, vanity and revenge), and through five duties: to self, family, society, nation and world. The method is presented as secular, experimental and open to anyone who wants to test it.

He explained karma in physical terms. A paper by Palani and Sundaramoorthy (2018) says he related it to the circulation of magnetic energy, the pressure at the centre of a rotating field. The paper sums up his view in the line "Each person is the sculptor of their life; their thoughts are the chisel." Wikipedia reports his theory of space, matter and gravity, and attributes it to one book.

### Questions raised

No controversy about his conduct appears in reliable sources. The claims of health benefit appear on pages of teaching organisations, with no studies cited. His physical theories are his own and have no independent scientific evaluation on record. Figures such as 1,500 meditation centres come from the organisation.

## Lineage

Vethathiri sits at the edge of the Tamil Siddha and Vallalar currents and outside the north Indian Kriya and Hatha lines of this part.
