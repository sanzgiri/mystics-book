*This epilogue will be written last, once every chapter's "inherited and new" verdict is in. What follows is the planned outline.*

## 1. The shape of the tree

What the finished graph looks like: how many roots and trunks it has, and where branches cross. A first look at which figures are most connected and which stand alone.

## 2. Five real innovations

A ranked list, argued from the chapter verdicts. Early candidates: Ramana's inquiry as a practice of attention; Aurobindo's evolutionary spirituality; Krishnamacharya's postural yoga; Ambedkar's social reading of suffering; Krishnamurti's rejection of the teacher.

## 3. The great restatements

Figures whose great gift was translating, organizing and passing on older ideas, and why that work mattered as much as invention.

## 4. The Vivekananda bottleneck

How much of what the world now calls "Indian spirituality" passed through one reframing in the 1890s: universal, experiential, scientific, non-sectarian. What it made possible, and what it left out.

## 5. Lineage and legitimacy

Patterns in claimed versus documented edges: immortal masters, reincarnations, visionary initiations. Which figures needed a lineage and which refused one.

## 6. The question that remains

A closing, first-person reflection on the thirteen questions the book asks, and which of them the tree answers.
