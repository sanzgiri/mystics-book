For most Hindus in 1800 the Vedas were the foundation of religion and almost nobody read them. They were held to be eternal and authorless, recited by Brahmins in a language few others understood, and known to everyone else through the priests, the Puranas and the temple. The people in this part asked whether that arrangement could survive being looked at directly. They took the oldest texts out of the hands of the specialists, put them into print and into Bengali, Hindi and English, and asked what the texts actually said and what in them a reasonable person could believe.

Rammohan Roy started it in Calcutta between 1815 and 1820, translating the Upanishads and arguing that they taught the worship of one formless God and gave no warrant for images. Debendranath Tagore turned his small society into the Brahmo Samaj, sent scholars to Benares to study the four Vedas, and concluded around 1850 that they were not free from error, so that the purified heart had to judge them. Keshab Chandra Sen went further and made every religion's scriptures part of one continuing revelation. Dayananda Saraswati, a wandering ascetic from Gujarat, came to Calcutta in 1872, met the Brahmo leaders and rejected their conclusion. For him the four Vedic hymn collections were perfect and complete, and the error lay in everything written after them. Jyotirao Phule, in Poona, appears in a shorter sidebar. He read the same texts as the record of a conquest by Brahmins and saw nothing in them worth saving.

They disagreed about where authority sits once the priest is set aside. Rammohan put it in reason working on the Upanishads, Debendranath in the intuition of the worshipper, Keshab in an inspiration that never stopped and that he felt in himself, and Dayananda in the Vedic Samhitas read by strict grammatical rules. Phule put it in a plain test of whether a religion treats people as equals. All of them were sure image worship was a mistake, and all of them wanted religion to be something an ordinary person could read and argue about.

I have kept social reform in the background here, although these men were famous for it, because what interests me is how each one decided which words were God's. Read Rammohan first, since the others are answering him.
