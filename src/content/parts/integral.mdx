The older Indian traditions mostly assume that the spirit is already complete. The Self in the Upanishads is unchanging, and liberation means recognising what was always there; the world turns in cycles and goes nowhere in particular. In the decades after Darwin, a handful of Indian thinkers asked whether that was the whole story. If life evolves, perhaps consciousness evolves too, and perhaps the divine itself is still unfolding in the world.

Sri Aurobindo gave the boldest answer, and he has the chapter here. He taught that spirit first hid itself in matter and is now climbing back out of it, through life and mind toward a Supermind that would transform the body and the earth, with humanity as a stage on the way. Rabindranath Tagore, who visited him in Pondicherry in 1928, arrived at a gentler version through poetry and the songs of the Bauls, in which God becomes real in human beings and humanity grows toward a universal person. Muhammad Iqbal, writing from inside Islam, called the universe unfinished and asked the human self to grow strong enough to become God's co-worker. K. C. Bhattacharyya, a philosopher in Calcutta, described the self as freedom won by stages from everything it had mistaken for itself. Gopi Krishna, a government clerk in Kashmir, went through a kundalini awakening in 1937 and spent the rest of his life arguing that it was the biological engine of human evolution.

They disagree on almost everything that matters. For Aurobindo the evolution is cosmic and will change matter itself; for Gopi Krishna it is physiological and happens in the brain; for Tagore it is the moral and imaginative growth of humanity; for Bhattacharyya it is a logical ascent within reflection, with no claim about history. They also divide over the self. Iqbal wanted it strengthened and attacked the mystics who sought to dissolve it. Aurobindo kept Advaita's silent Brahman as one stage of a longer journey, and Bhattacharyya treated it as a form of freedom the subject reaches by itself.

I have grouped them because each took the idea of evolution seriously enough to change an inherited teaching. Read the Aurobindo chapter first, since the profiles are easiest to follow as replies to him.
