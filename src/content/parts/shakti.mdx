We usually think of awakening as something a person earns, slowly, by their own effort, the way one learns a language. The traditions in this part start from another idea. They speak of *shakti*, the power of the goddess that animates the world, and of *kundalini*, that power coiled asleep in each body. The question is whether this force can be passed from one person to another, so that the awakening comes first and the effort follows.

Swami Lakshmanjoo, who kept the teaching of Kashmir Shaivism alive in twentieth-century Srinagar, held that grace descends from Shiva in nine grades, owes nothing to merit, and may use a teacher as its channel, and he still asked his students to learn Sanskrit and meditate every day. Swami Muktananda received what he called divine initiation from Bhagavan Nityananda at Ganeshpuri on 15 August 1947, and later gave shaktipat, the guru's awakening touch, to whole halls of people in India and America. Shri Mataji Nirmala Devi, a lawyer's daughter from a Christian family in Chhindwara, offered kundalini awakening free and en masse, and her followers worship her as the Adi Shakti. P.R. Sarkar, a railway accountant at Jamalpur, taught tantra as a universal science and tied it to a programme for remaking the economy. Bamakhepa, the saint of the cremation ground at Tarapith, stands for the older Shakta tantra of Bengal that the others drew on in different ways.

They disagree about where the power lives and who controls it. For Lakshmanjoo it belongs to Shiva and a teacher only passes it on. Muktananda and Shri Mataji placed it in the person of the guru, and their movements grew around that person, with consequences that the chapters and profiles record. Sarkar kept the guru at the centre while insisting that the method was a science anyone could test. Gopi Krishna, a Kashmiri clerk who appears in Part 4, had a kundalini awakening with no guru at all and spent the rest of his life arguing that it was a biological process. The theme also runs through Part 5, where Lahiri Mahasaya and Yogananda pass on Kriya Yoga by initiation.

I have reported experiences of the force as the people who had them described them, and controversies with dates and court findings where they exist. I think the part reads best with Lakshmanjoo first, since his tradition supplies the vocabulary the others use.
