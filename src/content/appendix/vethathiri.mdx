Vethathiri Maharishi was born on 14 August 1911 in Guduvancheri, near Madras, the eighth child of a family Wikipedia describes as weavers. Poverty ended his schooling early. He worked as an apprentice weaver, later owned a textile business, and qualified as a practitioner of Siddha medicine, Ayurveda and homeopathy. By the movement's account the seer Paranjyothi initiated him into kundalini yoga, and he drew inspiration from the nineteenth-century Tamil saint Vallalar. In 1958 he founded the World Community Service Centre in Chennai, which describes itself as non-profit and non-religious, and in 1984 the Arivu Thirukkoil, the Temple of Consciousness, at Aliyar in Coimbatore district. He wrote some 70 to 75 books, mostly in Tamil, and died in Coimbatore on 28 March 2006.

He held that suffering comes from a failure to understand oneself and one's relation to others, and his movement states his aim as world peace through individual peace. He explained karma in physical terms, relating it to the circulation of magnetic energy. His system presents old Tamil material as a short graded course for householders, secular and open to anyone who wants to test it. Its stages run from the awakening of kundalini at the base of the spine to the crown centre, through the mastery of six faults and through five duties, to self, family, society, nation and world.

**Lineage.** He sits at the edge of the Tamil Siddha and Vallalar currents; the tie to Vallalar is one of inspiration, and the tie to Paranjyothi rests on the movement's account.

**Practice.** Simplified Kundalini Yoga: simple physical exercises, meditation, introspection to analyse thoughts and neutralise anger, and kayakalpa, an old south Indian rejuvenation practice.
