Siddharameshwar Maharaj was born at Pathri, near Solapur, in the Marathi-speaking Deccan, and little is recorded of his early life. In 1906, aged about eighteen, he was initiated by Bhausaheb Maharaj of Inchegiri, who taught meditation on the divine name for hours a day over many years, a method his followers called the ant's way. After Bhausaheb died in 1914, Siddharameshwar began to doubt that long meditation was necessary. Around 1920 he started teaching the bird's way, the *vihangam marg*, in which the student reaches the Self directly through understanding. Several fellow disciples opposed the change, and he went on alone.

His method was discrimination, taught line by line from the *Dasbodh* of Samarth Ramdas. His listeners examined the gross body, the subtle body of mind and thought, the causal body of deep sleep and the great causal body of pure knowing, and found each to be something observed. What remains is the Self, which he placed beyond even the knowledge "I am." He kept the mantra of his line and treated meditation as an aid to understanding. He died of an infection on 9 November 1936, at forty-eight. Nisargadatta Maharaj, who had been with him for about two and a half years, collected and published his talks in Marathi in 1961 and 1962.

**Lineage.** He stands third in the Inchegiri line after Nimbargi Maharaj and Bhausaheb Maharaj, and his disciples included Nisargadatta Maharaj and Ranjit Maharaj; the meditative branch of Bhausaheb's teaching continued separately through R.D. Ranade.

**Practice.** Initiation with the mantra of the line, followed by inquiry through the four bodies of the *Dasbodh* until the student recognises the observer as the Self.
