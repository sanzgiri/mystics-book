Dada Bhagwan was born Ambalal Muljibhai Patel on 7 November 1908 in Tarsali, a village near Baroda. He was a contractor who lived an ordinary householder's life and kept his business. By his own account, repeated in the movement's booklets, self-realisation came to him on a June evening in 1958 as he sat on a bench at Surat railway station, over the next forty-eight minutes. He said this was the source of his knowledge, and no human guru is recorded. He began giving what his followers call *gnan*, knowledge, to others; the first public ceremony took place in Bombay in 1968, and his discourses, recorded between 1974 and 1988, were later edited into the *Aptavani* volumes. He died on 2 January 1988.

His teaching is called Akram Vignan, the stepless science. It sets itself against *kram*, the step-by-step Jain path of renunciation, discipline and purification, and promises liberation through a short ceremony, the Gnan Vidhi, with the grace of Simandhar Swami, in Jain belief a living tirthankara in a distant realm. In the movement's usage the name Dada Bhagwan refers to the Lord within, which he said was fully manifest in him and present in all living beings. The scholar Peter Flügel reads the movement as a new combination of Jain and Hindu devotional ideas.

**Lineage.** The tree links him to the Jain tradition as an influence; after his death the succession divided between Kanubhai Patel and Niruben Amin, whose side became the Dada Bhagwan Foundation.

**Practice.** After the Gnan Vidhi, followers live as householders and observe the self as separate from the body and mind.
