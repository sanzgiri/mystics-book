## Sentences I was unsure about

- epilogue.mdx, section 6 opening: "The book set out expecting the tree to answer several of them, and it answers fewer than hoped." The original expectation was the author's; it is now attributed to the book. Alternative if the author prefers less personification: "The tree answers fewer of them than the book's design anticipated."
- epilogue.mdx, final sentence: "The book began as a search for the innovators, and it ends with more respect than expected for the people who carried old teachings carefully." Keeps the admission of a changed view; "than expected" now has no explicit subject.
- direct-path.mdx: "Of all the parts, this one proved the hardest to write and the most interesting." A claim about the writing process now in the book's voice; it could be cut if the author finds it odd without an "I".
- grace.mdx: "This part is the hardest to judge by the book's own measure" drops the hedge of "I find". Direct-path says "hardest to write" and grace "hardest to judge", so the two do not collide.
- Generic "you" in the openings of conduct, direct-path and grace ("You keep your promises", "You purify your conduct", "You inquire, or breathe") was kept: it describes a practice, not the narrator, and the brief allows reader address where natural.
