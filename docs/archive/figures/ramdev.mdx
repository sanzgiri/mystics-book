## Life

Ramdev was born Ram Kisan Yadav in Alipur, a village in Haryana's Mahendragarh district, to farming parents, Ram Niwas and Gulabo Devi. The date most often given is 25 December 1965, but his birth year is uncertain and some accounts put it as late as the mid-1970s.

He went to a government school until about the eighth standard. Reading *Satyarth Prakash*, the founding text of Dayananda Saraswati's Arya Samaj, turned him towards the traditional schooling the Samaj promoted, and he studied Sanskrit and the Vedic texts in gurukuls in Haryana, including Arsh Gurukul Mahavidyalaya in the 1980s. In these years he met Balkrishna, who became his lifelong partner in business and Ayurveda. In the early 1990s he moved to Haridwar, to Kripalu Bagh Ashram in Kankhal, where he took the vows of a sannyasi from its head, Swami Shankar Dev, and the name Ramdev.

On 5 January 1995 Ramdev, Balkrishna and Acharya Karamveer, who had taught Ramdev yoga, founded the Divya Yog Mandir Trust in Haridwar. Ramdev began appearing on the devotional channel Sanskar TV in 2002, and in 2003 Aastha TV gave him its early-morning yoga slot. The format, a saffron-robed monk leading a stadium-sized crowd through breathing exercises, made him a household figure. In 2008 *Mail & Guardian* reported that more than twenty million people watched him daily. His residential camps drew large crowds, and in 2006 he and Balkrishna founded Patanjali Ayurved. Balkrishna holds most of the shares; Ramdev, as a renunciate, holds none and is the company's public face.

## The teaching

Ramdev presents yoga as the system of the sage Patanjali, whose name his institutions carry, and he says it is a complete science of body, mind and spirit. "Yoga is self-healing and self-realisation," he told the *Mail & Guardian* in 2008. In practice his emphasis falls almost wholly on the breath; a teacher of his method told *Gulf News* in 2008 that Ramdev places ninety per cent of the stress on breathing.

The daily routine he teaches is a set of seven breathing exercises that can be done at home, without a teacher, in under an hour. Two are the core. *Anulom vilom* is alternate-nostril breathing, closing one nostril and then the other. *Kapalbhati*, "skull-shining," is a series of sharp, forced exhalations from the belly. Others include *bhramari*, a humming exhale with the ears closed, and *udgeeth*, a long chanted *Om*. He wrote these up in *Pranayama Rahasya* (2009).

What set the teaching apart was its promise of health. Ramdev has said for two decades that regular pranayama can treat or cure a long list of conditions, from asthma, diabetes and high blood pressure to thyroid disease, hepatitis and, in his strongest claims, cancer. In 2008 he cited testimonies collected from his followers as evidence of cancer cures; he did not cite controlled trials. He joins this to a programme of national renewal: a disciplined, vegetarian, self-reliant India using its own medicine and its own products.

## Lineage

<MiniTree id="ramdev" />

Ramdev took sannyasa from **Swami Shankar Dev** of Kripalu Bagh Ashram and learned yoga from **Acharya Karamveer**, an Arya Samaj teacher, but he does not trace his teaching through a long guru line of the kind most figures in this book carry. His education links him to **Dayananda Saraswati**'s Arya Samaj, and his practice to the old yoga traditions in general. His closest associate is **Acharya Balkrishna**, managing director of Patanjali Ayurved.

## Critiques and questions

**Medical claims.** Doctors and rationalists have challenged his cure claims since the 2000s. In 2008 the Indian Medical Association's president called his cancer and HIV claims false hope for the sick. In June 2020 Patanjali launched Coronil as a treatment for Covid-19. The cure claim was later withdrawn. In 2021 Ramdev called modern medicine a "stupid science" in a video; he withdrew the remark and apologised after the health minister wrote to him.
